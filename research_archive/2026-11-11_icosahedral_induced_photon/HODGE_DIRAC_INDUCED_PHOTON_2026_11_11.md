# The Hodge–Dirac complex as matter-selected vacuum, and its parameter-free photon kernel

Prior work: none in the archive on flux selection or on kernels of the incidence graph (searched: Lieb, flux phase, logdet).

1. **Vacuum selection.** On the vertex–edge–face incidence graph of each Platonic solid, with a free U(1) phase on all
   4E incidences, the half-filled sea energy −Tr|B(θ)| is minimal exactly at flux π through every incidence square,
   i.e. at d1 d0 = 0. This is Lieb's flux-phase theorem (planar bipartite graph, four-sided faces); the certificate
   confirms it by minimisation from random starts and by the Hessian (PSD, null space = gauge).
2. **No free scale.** The incidence numbers are ±1, so the kernel K_E (Hessian of −Tr|B|) has no adjustable coupling.
   The log-det kernel K_L is scale-free as well.
3. **Spectra** on the (squares − 1) physical modes: positive definite on all five solids; dual solids agree.
   Tetrahedron exactly: K = 1/8 (3), 1/4 (3), 3/8 (5), Tr K = 3, Tr K⁻¹ = 148/3.
   Tr K_E: 3, 6.40004, 6.40004, 16.06005, 16.06005 (aggregate 47.92018 on 175 modes).
4. **Reduction to the 19 link-flux modes of the icosahedron** (the modes whose Maxwell propagator gives 15 Tr M⁺ = 137),
   with the remaining incidence modes relaxed: K = 0.013755 (3), 0.059279 (5), 0.073655 (4₃), 0.087843 (4₅), 0.088735 (3′)
   against M = 3−√5, 2, 3, 5, 3+√5. κ = K/M = 0.01801, 0.02964, 0.02455, 0.01757, 0.01695: positive in every sector,
   within a factor 1.75, not constant. 15 Tr K⁺ = 6541.40.
   The reduction with the other modes frozen is not gauge invariant and is not a photon kernel.
