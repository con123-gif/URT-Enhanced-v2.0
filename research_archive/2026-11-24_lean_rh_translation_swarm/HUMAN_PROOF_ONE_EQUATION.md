## The equation

Write Λ(n)=log p when n=p^j for a prime p and integer j>=1, and zero otherwise. Let γ denote Euler's constant. The central statement of the pinned proof, specialized to modulus one, is

    [E]  G(x) := sum_{n<=x} Λ(n)(x/n-1) - x log x + (1+γ)x
                <= x^(1/2+9ε).

Quantifiers: there exists ε0>0 such that for every 0<ε<ε0 and every Y0>=1 there is Y>=Y0 for which [E] holds for every real x in [Y,Y^(3/2)]. The coefficient 9 and the exponent 3/2 are from the actual native path (not an optimization, approximation or fit). ε0 can be decreased, in particular below 1/52.

## 1. Why the original conclusion is exactly [E]

A primitive Dirichlet character is a character of least conductor. The native cyclic level generated by a primitive character ψ is formed by taking the primitive representatives of powers of ψ. For ψ=the modulus-one character 1, all its powers equal 1; the primitive representative is again 1. Thus KEPT={1}, the size of the cyclic subgroup is o=1, and there are no nonprincipal terms in Level.Gm. The source identity Gz_eq identifies Formalism.Gz, An.G and Deduction.G, so this Level.Gm is precisely the left-hand side of [E], not an auxiliary prime-only expression. The native level_family lemma uses the positive weight 1 for the principal character; consequently its finite character family specializes to one character of modulus one.

The source TopPlug.zero_free_all_of_green_v_win fixes the window exponent la=3/2. It invokes the actual shape_closed_link_n_of_green and supplyChi, NOT the alternate plugAll path. In TopShape.shape_closed_link_n_of_green the call to topDesWin supplies eD=2 and eL=0 (the preceding 2 and 3 are Cabs and C0). Hence the FamilyClassConnected output provides fixed exponents 2,0 and a positive ε0. TopWin and core_gm produce the exponent 1/2+(eD+eL+7)ε=1/2+9ε. This is the original proof's route, conditional here on the native upstream producers. No claim that all auxiliary character bounds can be discarded merely because KEPT is a singleton.

## 2. The arithmetic-geometric calculation that yields [E]

At a given admissible scale, the native construction supplies a finite abelian group H of order n, a subgroup A0, finite subsets TT and T2⊆A0, m lift levels and positive b,k. Write r=|TT|, d=|T2|/n, Q=rmb; the actual size hypotheses imply Q,d>0. The arithmetic data include the Artin/Frobenius maps, a function ell of the characters (matching the primitive logarithmic derivatives), heights a(t) and weights w(u,j,ρ). All are the same host and same family members; independent choices would not justify the calculation.

Let F=|PhiSet|=2n. For each u in A0 and t in TT define the native thick Fourier sum
    Th(u,t)= (b/F) sum_{s in T2} sum_χ χ(-e1(s+u-t)) GPsi(χ,a(t)/b),
where e1(h)=((1,0),h). The native balance identity ASK−MAIN=De is just finite Fourier inversion at the actual complementary colors g. To see its weight comparison directly, put v_g=w(partner(t,g)), A=a(t), and define

Primitive reading and ramified orthogonality give P_g(v)=sum_l Lambda(l) K(l,g)(A/l-v)_+ -(A/F)log(A/v)+C_g, with C_g independent of v. For l=p^i the kernel K is 2/|I_p| on the coset i*frob(p)+I_p and zero elsewhere, where I_p is the inertia subgroup; it is nonnegative and sums to 2. Thus no cancellation is lost at ramified primes. De.re is the sum of P_g(v_g) over complementary colors; Th.re is its thick subset evaluated at b. On thick colors b<=v_g<=b+eta+1, the positive-part difference is at most (v_g-b)1_{l<A/b}, and the log term in P_g(b)-P_g(v_g) is nonpositive. Summing first over g gives a total thick cost <=2(eta+1)psi(A/b), where psi(y)=sum_{l<=y}Lambda(l). The elementary Chebyshev bound psi(y)<=4(log 2)y follows by applying prime-power valuations to the central binomial coefficients and summing dyadically. Since A/b<=x+1, the cost is <=6(x+1)(eta+1). On thin colors v_g<=2b', A/v_g>=X'/3 by b'X'<=xb, A/b>=x-1, x>=3; ThinScales gives P_g(v_g)>=-2A theta. There are at most F such colors. ## 3. Why [E] establishes the Riemann hypothesis

For Re s>2, let Φ(s)=integral_1^∞ G(x)x^(-s-1) dx. Absolute convergence follows from Λ(n)<=log n and elementary bounds. Interchange sum and integral; each summand contributes n^(-s)/(s(s-1)), since integral_n^∞ (x/n-1)x^(-s-1) dx = n^(-s)/(s(s-1)). The Euler product logarithmic derivative gives
    Φ(s) = -ζ'(s)/(s(s-1)ζ(s)) - 1/(s-1)^2 + (1+γ)/(s-1).
The Laurent expansion ζ(s)=1/(s-1)+γ+O(s-1) shows the apparent singularity at 1 cancels. This is the singleton instance of native mellin_Gchi_modOne and poleData_char. At a nontrivial zero ρ of multiplicity mρ, the residue is -mρ/[ρ(ρ-1)]≠0. In particular no other character can cancel it in the singleton family. For real zeros between 0 and 1 this residue would be positive. The zeros are isolated, and Φ is analytic in the positive half-plane away from those zeros.

The general native real-pole case disappears at modulus one: there is no positive real zero of zeta. Indeed for 0<s<1 the alternating eta series is positive by grouping successive pairs, and eta(s)=(1-2^(1-s))zeta(s). For s>1 use the Euler product, and at 1 the pole of the transform has already cancelled.

Here is the fixed-window Landau argument in full (peer 5dc8ff3a). Suppose [E] holds; set theta=1/2+9epsilon<1, U=log Y and g(u)=G(exp u). Then g(u)<=exp(theta u) on [U,3U/2] and |g(u)|<=C_0 exp(2u) for u>=0. Choose sigma=336, k=ceil(420U), U>=1, E=sigma-theta, and A_k(z)=(-1)^k Phi^(k)(z)/k!. Differentiation under the integral is justified by exponential decay. Writing W(u)=u^k exp(-sigma*u)/k!, the triangle inequality applied to the positive and negative parts separately gives the exact one-sided bound
    |A_k(sigma+it)|+Re A_k(sigma) <= 2 integral_0^infty max(g,0) W.
On the window, exp(theta*u)W<=E^(-k) by the exponential series. Off the window, bound the positive part by C_0 exp(2u); on the right multiply the integrand by exp(14(u-3U/2))>=1, on the left by exp(14(U-u))>=1. Extending each integral to the whole half-line gives a tail bounded by C_0 T_k, where
    T_k=exp(-21U)/320^(k+1)+exp(14U)/348^(k+1).
Using log(21/20)<=1/20, log(28/29)<=-1/29 and 420U<=k<420U+1 gives 336^k T_k<=exp(1/20)/320+1/348=C_tail. Consequently, for every real t on the same chosen windows,
    |A_k(sigma+it)|+Re A_k(sigma) <= (U+2C_0 C_tail) E^(-k).

If a zero rho0 has Re rho0>theta, let D=min(E, nearest pole distance from sigma). A minimum below E is attained, since closed disks of radius E lie in Re>0 and contain only finitely many poles. If D=E select rho1=rho0; otherwise select a nearest pole rho1 to sigma. No pole is real, so the horizontal distance sigma-Re rho1 is strictly less than D in both cases. Move the center vertically to sigma+i Im rho1. Its distance to rho1 is <D; a small vertical perturbation avoids the finitely many nearest-pole ties in a slightly larger disk, giving a unique nearest pole rho at distance d<D. (All poles are strictly left of sigma; distinct poles cannot be equidistant at every height.) Choose d<r<=D smaller than every other pole distance at the shifted center z and put e=(d+r)/2. The real-centered D-disk is holomorphic; the shifted r-disk is holomorphic after subtracting c/(s-rho), with c its nonzero residue. Cauchy's estimates on radius e and the reverse triangle inequality give
    |A_k(z)|+Re A_k(sigma) >= (|c|/d)d^(-k)-M_1 e^(-k)
for a fixed M_1. Combining the estimates and multiplying by d^k yields
    |c|/d <= M_1(d/e)^k+(U+2C_0 C_tail)(d/E)^k -> 0,
a contradiction since 0<d<e<=E and U->infty, k>=U. This proves the singleton Landau implication for the actual 3/2 windows, without invoking the generic real-pole dichotomy. Choose epsilon sufficiently small so theta<Re rho0 for each hypothetical zero with real part >1/2. Reflection by the zeta functional equation excludes those with real part <1/2. Thus [E] implies RH. For the principal host, the conductor N=2^(E+2) q1 d with E>=2 and distinct odd q1,d. CRT and the defining kernel H give the quotient C_(2^E)×C2×C2 (real dyadic factor and two quadratic factors). This deletion is local to the principal reconstruction; CaseIV for odd coefficient primes is confined to the trivial character, but its native full-length bound is retained.

A second simplification is obtained before bounding the prime sums. Fix t>=2 and M=N*t. Let C1 be the native uniform CaseI exponent, and C=max(CZ,CII). For odd primes l outside Bchi, the native redPrime/sumBack transfer bounds the plus-length sum by the sum of normalized finite character lengths. In CaseI, the total over all finite prime sets at any fixed weight e<t is <=M^C1. In the complement, l-1 divides 2e, so l=2j+1 with j|e. In particular l<=2t; the native individual bound (l*N*e)^C is <=M^(2C) because N>=2 and l*N*e<=2Nt²<=N²t². The original proof uses the looser M^(3C) and independently counts primes and weights. Instead count the exceptional (l,e) pairs jointly:
    # {(l,e): 1<=e<t, l odd prime, l-1 divides 2e}
      <= sum_{j=1}^{t-1} floor((t-1)/j)
      <= (t-1)(1+log(t-1)).
Each pair has at most N characters (or the smaller actual quotient order). Sum over the weights and use the native redPrime transfer, retaining all finiteness conclusions. For every finite prime set outside Bchi and 2, the weighted total of plus-lengths is therefore at most
    t*M^C1 + N*(t-1)*(1+log(t-1))*M^(2C)
    <= M^(C1+1)+M^(2C+2)
    <= M^(1+max(C1+1,2C+2)).
All constants are chosen before host, weights, primes and coefficient DVRs. No individual prime bound is substituted for the all-prime CaseI aggregate. This yields a direct human specialization of the native SumOut proof and eliminates a whole impossible case, while replacing two independent size counts by a divisor-incidence sum.
