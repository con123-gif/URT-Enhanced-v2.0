## The equation

Write Λ(n)=log p when n=p^j for a prime p and integer j>=1, and zero otherwise. Let γ denote Euler's constant. The central statement of the pinned proof, specialized to modulus one, is

    [E]  G(x) := sum_{n<=x} Λ(n)(x/n-1) - x log x + (1+γ)x
                <= x^(1/2+9ε).

Quantifiers: there exists ε0>0 such that for every 0<ε<ε0 and every Y0>=1 there is Y>=Y0 for which [E] holds for every real x in [Y,Y^(3/2)]. The coefficient 9 and the exponent 3/2 are from the actual native path (not an optimization, approximation or fit). ε0 can be decreased, in particular below 1/52.

## 1. Why the original conclusion is exactly [E]

A primitive Dirichlet character is a character of least conductor. The native cyclic level generated by a primitive character ψ is formed by taking the primitive representatives of powers of ψ. For ψ=the modulus-one character 1, all its powers equal 1; the primitive representative is again 1. Thus KEPT={1}, the size of the cyclic subgroup is o=1, and there are no nonprincipal terms in Level.Gm. The source identity Gz_eq identifies Formalism.Gz, An.G and Deduction.G, so this Level.Gm is precisely the left-hand side of [E], not an auxiliary prime-only expression. The native level_family lemma uses the positive weight 1 for the principal character; consequently its finite character family specializes to one character of modulus one.

The source TopPlug.zero_free_all_of_green_v_win fixes the window exponent la=3/2. It invokes the actual shape_closed_link_n_of_green and supplyChi, NOT the alternate plugAll path. In TopShape.shape_closed_link_n_of_green the call to topDesWin supplies eD=2 and eL=0 (the preceding 2 and 3 are Cabs and C0). Hence the FamilyClassConnected output provides fixed exponents 2,0 and a positive ε0. TopWin and core_gm produce the exponent 1/2+(eD+eL+7)ε=1/2+9ε. This is the original proof's route, conditional here on the native upstream producers. No claim that all auxiliary character bounds can be discarded merely because KEPT is a singleton.

## 2. The arithmetic-geometric calculation that yields [E]

At a given admissible scale, the native construction supplies a finite abelian group H of order n, a subgroup A0, finite subsets TT and T2⊆A0, m lift levels and positive b,k. Write r=|TT|, d=|T2|/n, Q=rmb; the actual size hypotheses imply Q,d>0. The arithmetic data include the Artin/Frobenius maps, a function ell of the characters (matching the primitive logarithmic derivatives), heights a(t) and weights w(u,j,ρ). All are the same host and same family members; independent choices would not justify the calculation.

Let F=|PhiSet|=2n. For each u in A0 and t in TT define the native thick Fourier sum
    Th(u,t)= (b/F) sum_{s in T2} sum_χ χ(-e1(s+u-t)) GPsi(χ,a(t)/b),
where e1(h)=((1,0),h). The native character balance identity identifies the arithmetic excess ASK−MAIN with the sum of its thick and remaining contributions. The native weight and thin-character windows (SizesA, ThinScales, CostWindows), together with positive weights, the correct cutoff N and HostFactsS, imply for each translation
    sum_t sum_{j=1}^m Re Th(u,t) <= gapA(u)+2Q/x.
Here gapA is exactly the sum of ASK−Re MAIN over t,j, not a statistical proxy. This is the summation of th_le and ch_balance. The numerical 2Q/x is obtained from the two clauses of CostWindows, as in ChCore.core.

The geometry supplies

for every u∈A0, with
    B = r aE(1+m²/k) + m(2nmb x^ε + |T2| k b x^(2ε)/n²),
where aE is the native scale a. Indeed ChTop identifies the arithmetic gap with D.ASK−D.Main; each_unit_bound_statement bounds it by ErrX5at; errX5at_le bounds this by x^(CErr ε) ErrText, where CErr=c.eDiv+c.eLoss+3<=5. Now average over A0. A finite character summed over a subgroup is zero unless it is trivial on that subgroup, when the sum is the subgroup order (multiply the sum by a nontrivial character value to prove the zero case). Since every s∈T2 lies in A0, this gives the exact identity
    mean_u Th(u,t) = (bd/2) sum_{χ trivial on A0} χ(e1(t)) GPsi(χ,a(t)/b).
Native ChCore.one_char and sum_vv use the host character estimates and Shares condition to bound the remaining sum below, after summing over t, by
    r [G(x) - SigA(x) - 4 x^(1/2+ε)].
SigA is the sum of the magnitudes of the unkept characters trivial on A0. The native GmA=Level.Gm and the principal singleton identification are essential for this substitution. This lower estimate uses the source's derivative-integral and shift bounds; it is not obtained from orthogonality alone.

Combining the pointwise upper bound, exact subgroup average and this character lower bound yields the single central inequality
    (Qd/2) [G(x)-SigA(x)-4 x^(1/2+ε)] <= x^(5ε) B + 2Q/x.

We can check the exponent directly in ordinary algebra. The source size windows give aE<=2xb, r>=n*x^(-ε/8)/2, d>=x^(-ε), x^(1/2-ε)<=m<=x^(1/2+ε/2), xm<=k<=n²*x^(1/2-ε). Divide by Qd/2. The first B term contributes at most 4x^(1/2+2ε)+4x^ε; the second at most 8x^(1/2+21ε/8); the last at most 4x^(1/2+9ε/8). Multiplying each by x^(5ε), adding the 4x^(1/2+ε) shift and 4/(xd) cost, and using x>=1, gives

For each fixed ε>0, enlarge the eventual threshold until x^(3ε/8)>=28; this proves G−SigA<=x^(1/2+8ε). This direct coarse calculation uses a slightly larger harmless eventual threshold than the native proposition_size_bound (which uses 26). No new asymptotic hypothesis is needed.

## 3. Why [E] establishes the Riemann hypothesis

For Re s>2, let Φ(s)=integral_1^∞ G(x)x^(-s-1) dx. Absolute convergence follows from Λ(n)<=log n and elementary bounds. Interchange sum and integral; each summand contributes n^(-s)/(s(s-1)), since integral_n^∞ (x/n-1)x^(-s-1) dx = n^(-s)/(s(s-1)). The Euler product logarithmic derivative gives
    Φ(s) = -ζ'(s)/(s(s-1)ζ(s)) - 1/(s-1)^2 + (1+γ)/(s-1).
The Laurent expansion ζ(s)=1/(s-1)+γ+O(s-1) shows the apparent singularity at 1 cancels. This is the singleton instance of native mellin_Gchi_modOne and poleData_char. At a nontrivial zero ρ of multiplicity mρ, the residue is -mρ/[ρ(ρ-1)]≠0. In particular no other character can cancel it in the singleton family. For real zeros between 0 and 1 this residue would be positive. The zeros are isolated, and Φ is analytic in the positive half-plane away from those zeros.

The native Landau window lemma says: a real measurable g(u)=G(exp u) of growth O(exp(2u)), with this Laplace transform and pole data, cannot have a pole of real part greater than θ (0<θ<1) if on arbitrarily distant windows [U,(1+δ)U], δ>0 fixed, it has a nonnegative majorant R with integral R(u) exp(-θu) du <= exp(η U) for every η>0. Its proof differentiates the Laplace transform k times, choosing k proportional to U so the positive kernel u^k exp(-σu)/k! concentrates in the window. For the complex derivative and real derivative together, the negative part cancels because |integral g exp(-itu) times kernel| + integral g times kernel <= 2 integral max(g,0) times kernel. The native landau_tails bound controls the two outside tails. Cauchy's estimates applied after removing the nearest simple pole give a lower bound c/d^k minus M/e^k with 0<d<e<=σ-θ; the positive real-pole case is handled by the positive residue. The window estimate bounds the same combination by C exp(η U)/(σ-θ)^k. Choosing η<log(e/d), and using k>=U→∞, gives a contradiction. This paragraph reconstructs the native argument at lemma level; the exact tail selection and nearest-pole dichotomy proofs are retained as named inputs and are to be expanded in the final appendix, not silently assumed.

Choose any hypothetical zero ρ with Re ρ>1/2, less than 1 (nonvanishing for Re s>=1 is standard). Take ε<min(ε0,(Re ρ-1/2)/9), and choose θ=1/2+9ε<Re ρ. With U=log Y, [E] gives g(u)<=exp(θu) on [U,3U/2]. Use R=exp(θu); its weighted integral is U/2<=exp(ηU) for sufficiently large U. Thus the native lemma forces Re ρ<=θ, a contradiction. This is exactly the mechanism of formB -> zeroFree_of_rpow_window -> lemmaL. Reflection in the functional equation sends an off-line nontrivial zero with Re<1/2 to one with Re>1/2. Hence all nontrivial zeros have real part 1/2. This last reflection is the previously native-compiled conditional rh_of_right / reduced_strip_root adapter.

## 4. The geometric inequality: explicit human derivation

Fix one translation and its finite nonempty word fibre W. Averages below use the positive native weights d_i, normalized to sum one. Write A=sum_t a(t), B_1=sum_t a(t)(log a(t)-1-gamma+LpL), R=sum_j mean_rho log w_jrho, q=log(pi/v), and P=cCount*(La+m²La/k). In the notation of the source,

    Main=m B_1-A R,  SDiv=m B_1-m A q,
    Lam5=R-m q+m lg-P.

Substitution cancels the two common logarithmic terms exactly:
    SDiv-A Lam5+m A lg=Main+A P.

Put t_i=log T_i, E_i=Ee_i, h_i=h(i), s=mean(muN_i-h_i), uF=max_i(muMaxN_i-h_i)-s>=0. Let P_+=cSlope*r*a*La, P_-=P_+*(1+m/k), C_0=cSl1*m*B0, E_0=m*A*lg, E_D=EDiv and Lo_i=Lo5(i). The original local hypotheses are the following (every indexed statement holds on the same word fibre):
    muCC+h_i-Fo <= SDiv-A t_i+E_D;
    |E_i-E_0|<=m*cE*B0;
    |(h_i+A t_i)-(h_j+A t_j)|<=cH*(r*a*La+m*B0);
    Lam5<=t_i<=cCount*m*L, |t_i-t_j|<=cCount*log k;
    -P_-<=muM_i, muMaxM_i<=P_+;
    muM_i+Ga+Fo-E_i-Lo_i<=muN_i<=muMaxN_i,
    muMaxN_i<=muMaxM_i+Ga+Fo-E_i+C_0.
The native loss is Lo_i=cSl1*IO+cSl1*r*m*(a/k+b')*t_i+cSl1*Xpp+1. The division error is E_D=cDiv*r*a*La+cDiv*m*(B0+Nc)*x^(eDiv*epsilon). The geometric source uses La (not L) in P.

Subtract the division inequality from the lower quotient-slope inequality and average. The lower count, exponent comparison and exact cancellation yield
    Ga-Main <= s-muCC+K_0+mean(Lo),
where K_0=A P+E_D+m*cE*B0+P_-. Independently set V=2m*cE*B0+cH*(r*a*La+m*B0)+A*cCount*log k. The three comparison bounds show that (-E_i-h_i)-(-E_j-h_j)<=V. Subtract the lower quotient bound at j from the upper one at i. Averaging j and maximizing i cancels Ga+Fo and gives
    uF<=K_3+mean(Lo),  K_3=P_++P_-+C_0+V.

The native entropy inequality (SlopeIneq) states
    s-muCC <= log|W|+
       [4uF log(1+HH/uF)+(eta+4sqrt(e))*HH]/(1-del).
Here 0<=del<=delBar<1, 0<=eta<=etaBar, 0<=e<=eBar<=1/4, and 0<HH<=X*b with X=x^cHH, together with s-muMinCC<=HH. For u>0, log z<=log(1+X)+z/(1+X)-1 applied to z=1+HH/u gives u log(1+HH/u)<=u log(1+X)+b X/(1+X). Since X/(1+X)<=log(1+X), the bound is <=(u+b)log(1+X), and the zero case follows by continuity. Use u=uF and the bound K_3+mean(Lo). The native losses ASK-Ga<=LoP and IO<=LoP, and the count upper bound, give
    mean(Lo)<=M_0:=cSl1*LoP+cSl1*r*m*(a/k+b')*cCount*m*L+cSl1*Xpp+1.
Consequently
    ASK-Main<=K_0+log|W|+LoP+M_0+
      [4log(1+X)*(K_3+M_0+b)+(etaBar+4sqrt(eBar))*X*b]/(1-delBar).
This is exactly ErrX5at (not a new substitute bound). Its size estimate is supplied by the native errX5at_le with ErrWindows and LargeX; its application to gapA uses the actual ASK/Main identification and BNBoundsU. The displayed algebra proves the local implication once these inequalities and the entropy premise are established. It does not establish the existence of their simultaneous geometric witnesses.
