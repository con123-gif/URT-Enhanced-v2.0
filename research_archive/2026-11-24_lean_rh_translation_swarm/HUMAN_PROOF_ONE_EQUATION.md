## The equation

Write Λ(n)=log p when n=p^j for a prime p and integer j>=1, and zero otherwise. Let γ denote Euler's constant. The central statement of the pinned proof, specialized to modulus one, is

    [E]  G(x) := sum_{n<=x} Λ(n)(x/n-1) - x log x + (1+γ)x
                <= x^(1/2+9ε).

Quantifiers: there exists ε0>0 such that for every 0<ε<ε0 and every Y0>=1 there is Y>=Y0 for which [E] holds for every real x in [Y,Y^(3/2)]. The coefficient 9 and the exponent 3/2 are from the actual native path (not an optimization, approximation or fit). ε0 can be decreased, in particular below 1/52.

## 3. Why [E] establishes the Riemann hypothesis

For Re s>2, let Φ(s)=integral_1^∞ G(x)x^(-s-1) dx. Absolute convergence follows from Λ(n)<=log n and elementary bounds. Interchange sum and integral; each summand contributes n^(-s)/(s(s-1)), since integral_n^∞ (x/n-1)x^(-s-1) dx = n^(-s)/(s(s-1)). The Euler product logarithmic derivative gives
    Φ(s) = -ζ'(s)/(s(s-1)ζ(s)) - 1/(s-1)^2 + (1+γ)/(s-1).
The Laurent expansion ζ(s)=1/(s-1)+γ+O(s-1) shows the apparent singularity at 1 cancels. This is the singleton instance of native mellin_Gchi_modOne and poleData_char. At a nontrivial zero ρ of multiplicity mρ, the residue is -mρ/[ρ(ρ-1)]≠0. In particular no other character can cancel it in the singleton family. For real zeros between 0 and 1 this residue would be positive. The zeros are isolated, and Φ is analytic in the positive half-plane away from those zeros.

The native Landau window lemma says: a real measurable g(u)=G(exp u) of growth O(exp(2u)), with this Laplace transform and pole data, cannot have a pole of real part greater than θ (0<θ<1) if on arbitrarily distant windows [U,(1+δ)U], δ>0 fixed, it has a nonnegative majorant R with integral R(u) exp(-θu) du <= exp(η U) for every η>0. Its proof differentiates the Laplace transform k times, choosing k proportional to U so the positive kernel u^k exp(-σu)/k! concentrates in the window. For the complex derivative and real derivative together, the negative part cancels because |integral g exp(-itu) times kernel| + integral g times kernel <= 2 integral max(g,0) times kernel. The native landau_tails bound controls the two outside tails. Cauchy's estimates applied after removing the nearest simple pole give a lower bound c/d^k minus M/e^k with 0<d<e<=σ-θ; the positive real-pole case is handled by the positive residue. The window estimate bounds the same combination by C exp(η U)/(σ-θ)^k. Choosing η<log(e/d), and using k>=U→∞, gives a contradiction. This paragraph reconstructs the native argument at lemma level; the exact tail selection and nearest-pole dichotomy proofs are retained as named inputs and are to be expanded in the final appendix, not silently assumed.

Choose any hypothetical zero ρ with Re ρ>1/2, less than 1 (nonvanishing for Re s>=1 is standard). Take ε<min(ε0,(Re ρ-1/2)/9), and choose θ=1/2+9ε<Re ρ. With U=log Y, [E] gives g(u)<=exp(θu) on [U,3U/2]. Use R=exp(θu); its weighted integral is U/2<=exp(ηU) for sufficiently large U. Thus the native lemma forces Re ρ<=θ, a contradiction. This is exactly the mechanism of formB -> zeroFree_of_rpow_window -> lemmaL. Reflection in the functional equation sends an off-line nontrivial zero with Re<1/2 to one with Re>1/2. Hence all nontrivial zeros have real part 1/2. This last reflection is the previously native-compiled conditional rh_of_right / reduced_strip_root adapter.
