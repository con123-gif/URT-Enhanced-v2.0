## The equation

Write Λ(n)=log p when n=p^j for a prime p and integer j>=1, and zero otherwise. Let γ denote Euler's constant. The central statement of the pinned proof, specialized to modulus one, is

    [E]  G(x) := sum_{n<=x} Λ(n)(x/n-1) - x log x + (1+γ)x
                <= x^(1/2+9ε).

Quantifiers: there exists ε0>0 such that for every 0<ε<ε0 and every Y0>=1 there is Y>=Y0 for which [E] holds for every real x in [Y,Y^(3/2)]. The coefficient 9 and the exponent 3/2 are from the actual native path (not an optimization, approximation or fit). ε0 can be decreased, in particular below 1/52.

A primitive Dirichlet character is a character of least conductor. The native cyclic level generated by a primitive character ψ is formed by taking the primitive representatives of powers of ψ. ## 3. Why [E] establishes the Riemann hypothesis

For Re s>2, let Φ(s)=integral_1^∞ G(x)x^(-s-1) dx. Absolute convergence follows from Λ(n)<=log n and elementary bounds. Interchange sum and integral; each summand contributes n^(-s)/(s(s-1)), since integral_n^∞ (x/n-1)x^(-s-1) dx = n^(-s)/(s(s-1)). The Euler product logarithmic derivative gives
    Φ(s) = -ζ'(s)/(s(s-1)ζ(s)) - 1/(s-1)^2 + (1+γ)/(s-1).
The Laurent expansion ζ(s)=1/(s-1)+γ+O(s-1) shows the apparent singularity at 1 cancels. This is the singleton instance of native mellin_Gchi_modOne and poleData_char. At a nontrivial zero ρ of multiplicity mρ, the residue is -mρ/[ρ(ρ-1)]≠0. In particular no other character can cancel it in the singleton family. For real zeros between 0 and 1 this residue would be positive. The zeros are isolated, and Φ is analytic in the positive half-plane away from those zeros.

The general native real-pole case disappears at modulus one: there is no positive real zero of zeta. Indeed for 0<s<1 the alternating eta series is positive by grouping successive pairs, and eta(s)=(1-2^(1-s))zeta(s). For s>1 use the Euler product, and at 1 the pole of the transform has already cancelled.

Here is the fixed-window Landau argument in full (peer 5dc8ff3a). Suppose [E] holds; set theta=1/2+9epsilon<1, U=log Y and g(u)=G(exp u). Then g(u)<=exp(theta u) on [U,3U/2] and |g(u)|<=C_0 exp(2u) for u>=0. Choose sigma=336, k=ceil(420U), U>=1, E=sigma-theta, and A_k(z)=(-1)^k Phi^(k)(z)/k!. Differentiation under the integral is justified by exponential decay. Writing W(u)=u^k exp(-sigma*u)/k!, the triangle inequality applied to the positive and negative parts separately gives the exact one-sided bound
    |A_k(sigma+it)|+Re A_k(sigma) <= 2 integral_0^infty max(g,0) W.
On the window, exp(theta*u)W<=E^(-k) by the exponential series. Off the window, bound the positive part by C_0 exp(2u); on the right multiply the integrand by exp(14(u-3U/2))>=1, on the left by exp(14(U-u))>=1. Extending each integral to the whole half-line gives a tail bounded by C_0 T_k, where
    T_k=exp(-21U)/320^(k+1)+exp(14U)/348^(k+1).
Using log(21/20)<=1/20, log(28/29)<=-1/29 and 420U<=k<420U+1 gives 336^k T_k<=exp(1/20)/320+1/348=C_tail. Consequently, for every real t on the same chosen windows,
    |A_k(sigma+it)|+Re A_k(sigma) <= (U+2C_0 C_tail) E^(-k).

If a zero rho0 has Re rho0>theta, let D=min(E, nearest pole distance from sigma). A minimum below E is attained, since closed disks of radius E lie in Re>0 and contain only finitely many poles. If D=E select rho1=rho0; otherwise select a nearest pole rho1 to sigma. No pole is real, so the horizontal distance sigma-Re rho1 is strictly less than D in both cases. Move the center vertically to sigma+i Im rho1. Its distance to rho1 is <D; a small vertical perturbation avoids the finitely many nearest-pole ties in a slightly larger disk, giving a unique nearest pole rho at distance d<D. (All poles are strictly left of sigma; distinct poles cannot be equidistant at every height.) Choose d<r<=D smaller than every other pole distance at the shifted center z and put e=(d+r)/2. The real-centered D-disk is holomorphic; the shifted r-disk is holomorphic after subtracting c/(s-rho), with c its nonzero residue. Cauchy's estimates on radius e and the reverse triangle inequality give
    |A_k(z)|+Re A_k(sigma) >= (|c|/d)d^(-k)-M_1 e^(-k)
for a fixed M_1. Combining the estimates and multiplying by d^k yields
    |c|/d <= M_1(d/e)^k+(U+2C_0 C_tail)(d/E)^k -> 0,
a contradiction since 0<d<e<=E and U->infty, k>=U. This proves the singleton Landau implication for the actual 3/2 windows, without invoking the generic real-pole dichotomy. Choose epsilon sufficiently small so theta<Re rho0 for each hypothetical zero with real part >1/2. Reflection by the zeta functional equation excludes those with real part <1/2. Thus [E] implies RH.
