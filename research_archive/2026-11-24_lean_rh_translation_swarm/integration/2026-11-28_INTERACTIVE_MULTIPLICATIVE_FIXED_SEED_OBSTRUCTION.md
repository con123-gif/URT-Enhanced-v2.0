# Fixed-seed multiplicative descent has an unavoidable positive residual floor

28 November 2026. Continuation of 7e4c01b46d57f5fdf012012e77b49ae29f8444e9 (exact note retrieved). This is an unconditional obstruction to the particular single-seed multiplicative algorithm, irrespective of RH. It does not contradict the previous theorem of strict finite descent or any finite improvement. It does not prove RH or its negation. No numerical computation, Lean replay, worker/schedule changes, or novelty claim.

## 1. Evaluation inequality
For a finite Dirichlet polynomial P(s)=sum_(n<=N) p_n n^(-s) with P(1)=0, let r(t)=1_(t>=1)+sum_(n>=2) p_n b_n(t), where b_n(t)={t/n}-{t}/n, and E(P)=integral_1^infinity |r(t)|^2 dt/t^2. The prior Mellin identity is
 integral_1^infinity r(t)t^(-s-1)dt=(1-zeta(s)P(s))/s.
It holds throughout Re s>0 by analytic continuation: r is bounded, the integral is holomorphic there, and the pole of zeta at 1 is removable in the product because P(1)=0. For s=beta+i gamma, beta>1/2, Cauchy-Schwarz in the measure dt/t^2 gives
 |1-zeta(s)P(s)|^2/|s|^2 <= E(P)/(2 beta-1).
In particular if P(s)=0 and s!=1, then E(P)>=(2 beta-1)/|s|^2>0, regardless of whether zeta(s) vanishes. The same inequality applies to all finite admissible updates.

## 2. Every nonzero finite admissible seed has infinitely many artificial obstructions
Let P_0 be a nonzero finite Dirichlet polynomial with P_0(1)=0. Choose 0<r<1/4 such that P_0 has no zeros on the circle |s-1|=r (isolated zeros). Put c=min_circle |P_0|>0. There exists a sequence T_j of positive integers tending to infinity such that exp(-i T_j log n)->1 for every n in the finite support. Elementary proof: the sequence (exp(-i m log n))_n for integer m>=1 lies in a compact finite-dimensional torus; take a convergent subsequence indexed by strictly increasing integers m_j. Then T_j=m_(2j)-m_j>=j, and the componentwise ratios tend to 1. No number-theoretic independence hypothesis is required.
Thus P_0(s+i T_j)->P_0(s) uniformly on the circle, since the defining sum is finite. For large j the difference has modulus <c there. Rouche's theorem gives at least one zero rho_j of P_0 in |rho_j-(1+i T_j)|<r. These zeros satisfy Re rho_j>3/4 and have unbounded imaginary parts. Passing to a further subsequence makes them distinct. In particular choose any such rho_* !=1.
The recurrence argument applies to every nonzero finite admissible seed, including an optimized multi-scale seed or a rationally certified witness. It does not require that rho_* be distinct from every zeta zero; if it is a zeta zero, the evaluation obstruction still holds. In fact there are artificial zeros that are not zeta zeros for suitable seeds, e.g. the explicit binary seed below, but no such distinction is needed for the general obstruction.

## 3. Multiplicative-only iteration cannot converge to zero
Let P_j=P_0 prod_(i=1)^j (1+B_i), with each B_i any finite Dirichlet polynomial. All P_j retain P_0(1)=0 and P_0(rho_*)=0. Section 1 gives the fixed positive bound
 E(P_j) >= (2 Re rho_*-1)/|rho_*|^2   for all j.
It also holds for any norm limit of their residuals because the evaluation functional is continuous. This refutes the possibility of proving zero convergence for this particular fixed-seed algorithm even assuming RH. Strict finite-step descent remains true but must be bounded below by a positive floor. The obstruction is independent of the size of each correction, number of scales, support cutoff, and computational complexity. The degenerate seed P_0=0 gives residual norm 1 and cannot move by multiplication.

## 4. Necessary redesign: independent additive generators
A finite update may instead use P_new=P+P_2 B_2+P_3 B_3, where P_b(s)=1-b^(1-s), b=2,3, and B_b are independent finite Dirichlet polynomials. P_new(1)=0, but zeros of P are not necessarily preserved. The common zeros of P_2 and P_3 are exactly s=1: their zeros have Re s=1 and Im s is respectively a multiple of 2pi/log 2 or 2pi/log 3, and log 2/log 3 is irrational by unique factorization. At s=1 the analytic extensions of zeta(s)P_b(s) take the nonzero value log b. Thus throughout Re s>1/2 the two analytic products zeta(s)P_b(s) have common zeros exactly at genuine zeta zeros (excluding the removable point 1). At any other point an additive constant multiple of at least one generator can change the residual evaluation. This removes the fixed-seed common-zero obstruction, not all possible approximation obstructions. In particular no theorem that the closed space generated by these two sets of multiples is dense has been proved; absence of common zeros is not by itself a density theorem.

The unrestricted additive space from the earlier matrix-free construction remains admissible and does not inherit a mandatory fixed P_0 factor. Prefer additive/multi-generator corrections instead of trying to extend the single-seed strict descent theorem to zero convergence. Do not assert a universal descent rate or claim that removing the artificial obstruction establishes RH. The main compact reconstruction and worker schedules remain unchanged.
