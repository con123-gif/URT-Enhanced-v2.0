## The actual mathematical mechanism
Let E/F be a CM quadratic extension unramified at every finite place. Let W and W0 be nondegenerate Hermitian spaces of the same positive rank n over E, each with a self-dual O_E lattice. At finite v normalize Haar measure so its lattice stabilizer K_v has volume one. At infinity normalize each real unitary group by the compact-dual density convention in DataCD. Then the two normalized global quotient volumes are equal. This is a consequence of the classical Tamagawa theorem, not RH, not an additional conjecture.

Proof. On the F-Lie algebra of U(W), take B(X,Y)=tr_E(XY) (ordinary matrix trace, valued in F by the skew-adjoint identities). Its scalar extension to E identifies with the perfect trace pairing on gl_n. In an F-basis b, let d=det(B(b_i,b_j)) in F^*. Define invariant absolute density at v to be |d|_v^(1/2) times the invariant top-form measure assigning coordinate density one in b at identity. This is basis-independent; the product formula for d identifies its global product, with the usual convergence and discriminant factors, with the Tamagawa measure. The convergence factors for the unitary group depend on its determinant norm-one torus, hence on E/F, not the Hermitian form or signature.

Real-place normalization: in standard complex matrix coordinates let A have the real basis b of u(p,q) as its columns after complexification. Since u(p,q) is a real form of gl_n(C), A is invertible over C. The Gram matrix of the complex matrix trace pairing is the permutation pairing of E_ij with E_ji, with determinant of absolute value one. Therefore |det B| = |det_C A|^2. The map (X,Y)->X+iY has real Jacobian |det_C A|^2 in these coordinates. If a Lebesgue density on the real Lie algebra has coefficient c in b-coordinates, its product pushes forward to standard complex-entry Lebesgue exactly when c^2=|det_C A|^2. Thus c=|det B|^(1/2), up to the same ambient volume convention constant for each signature of dimension n^2. Conjugation by any complex frame has determinant one on gl_n, so the native frame A_rho does not change this normalization. Consequently the Lie density used by IsOmegaLeb is precisely this trace-pairing density (up to that common convention). The small exponential-ball ratio in OmegaDensity identifies the scalar multiplying it for a Haar measure. CDHaar.trick equates this scalar with the compact group U(n), and CDHaar.prob fixes the latter. Hence the multiplier is the SAME for every real signature, without computing the compact-group volume explicitly.

Finite-place normalization: the integral unitary group of a perfect Hermitian lattice over an unramified quadratic (or split) extension is a smooth reductive O_v-group; after finite etale splitting it becomes GL_n, including at residue characteristic 2. Its integral Lie lattice becomes Mat_n, on which the trace pairing is perfect. Perfectness descends by faithful flatness, so in an integral basis d is a unit. The invariant density gives K_v volume #Gbar(F_q)/q^(n^2) by smooth reduction (q^(n^2) lifts per step). At split v the reductive group is GL_n; at nonsplit unramified v it is U_n. Their orders give in both cases
    mu_B(K_v)=prod_{j=1}^n (1-eta_v^j q_v^(-j)),
where eta_v=+1 at split places and -1 at inert places. In particular the factor is the SAME for W and W0. The determinant torus convergence factors are likewise the same. No division by a conditionally convergent unregularized Euler product: compare the standard Tamagawa-regularized products, whose factors agree place by place.

Finally tau(U(W))=tau(U(W0))=2 (the classical unitary Tamagawa number, using the simply connected special unitary derived group and the quadratic norm-one torus). Both normalized quotient volumes are therefore this same number times the same archimedean, finite and global normalization factors. This proves signature independence in ordinary adelic mathematics. It actually applies to any real signatures, not only the selected pair.
