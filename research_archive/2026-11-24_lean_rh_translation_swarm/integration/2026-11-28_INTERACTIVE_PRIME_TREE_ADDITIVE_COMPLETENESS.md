# Prime-tree additive redesign: exact finite completeness and stable linear coefficient transform

28 November 2026. Continuation of 3e07791b7b35ecd91e441886738d3c2c4b4bca39 (exact source retrieved). This removes an algebraic restriction of the two-generator redesign. It is a proved finite coefficient-space result and a tested linear algorithm, NOT a proof of geometric density of the residual space, RH, or a new zero-free region. No changes to workers/schedules; no native Lean replay or priority claim.

## 1. Two generators are not algebraically complete
Identify finite Dirichlet polynomials with finite multivariate polynomials in variables X_p=p^(-s) for primes p. Injectivity follows from unique factorization and linear independence of distinct Dirichlet monomials (e.g. take s real tending to infinity and isolate the smallest nonzero index). The condition P(1)=0 means evaluation at X_p=1/p is zero.
The ideal generated by 1-2X_2 and 1-3X_3 vanishes under substitution X_2=1/2, X_3=1/3, keeping all other variables free. In particular it cannot contain 1-5X_5, although this is admissible. Thus the two-generator construction does not generate all finite admissible polynomials algebraically. This does not prove a restriction in the Hilbert-space closure, which is a different question.

## 2. Exact triangular prime-tree basis
For n>=2 let p(n) be its least prime factor, m(n)=n/p(n), e_n(s)=n^(-s)-1/n, e_1=0. Then exactly
 e_n=m(n)^(-s)e_{p(n)}+e_{m(n)}/p(n).
Set h_n=m(n)^(-s)e_{p(n)}. The N-1 functions h_n, n=2,...,N, form a basis of the admissible Dirichlet polynomials of length <=N: the transition to e_n is triangular with diagonal 1 and every parent index smaller than its child. This uses all primes up to N, not just 2,3. Every h_n is a single admissible prime generator times a monomial, with no fixed common polynomial factor. Every finite admissible polynomial can therefore be reached by a finite additive correction from any other admissible polynomial.

Given P=sum_{n=2}^N a_n e_n, process n=N,...,2 in descending order. Start c_n=a_n; output c_n as the h_n coefficient, and add c_n/p(n) to c_{m(n)} if m(n)>=2. At index 1 the e_1 term vanishes. This gives P=sum c_n h_n exactly. The inverse has a single pass subtracting c_n/p(n) from its parent coefficient. A linear Euler sieve for least prime factors, plus these passes, takes O(N) arithmetic operations and O(N) storage. This is not an arbitrary-precision bit complexity result.

## 3. Uniform unweighted coefficient stability
Define S on vectors indexed by 2,...,N by (Sc)_m=sum_{n: m(n)=m} c_n/p(n). Then a=(I-S)c. Each child belongs to exactly one row, and within any row its primes are distinct. Cauchy-Schwarz and disjointness of rows give ||S||_(2->2)^2 <= sum_p 1/p^2 <= sum_{n>=2}1/n^2 <= 1/4+integral_2^infinity x^-2 dx=3/4. Therefore the inverse finite triangular transform obeys
 ||c||_2 <= (1-sqrt(3)/2)^(-1)||a||_2,
 ||a||_2 <= (1+sqrt(3)/2)||c||_2,
uniformly in N. This proves a stable linear-time change of coordinates in the UNWEIGHTED coefficient norm. It does NOT bound the Nyman-Beurling geometric norm or the condition number of the geometric optimization. One cannot use these inequalities as a convergence proof.

In real t coordinates the corresponding correction basis is H_n(t)=T_{m(n)}b_{p(n)}(t)=b_n(t)-b_{m(n)}(t)/p(n). Its geometric norm is <=sqrt(2/n): b_p(t)=floor(t)/p for 1<=t<p and is bounded by 1 for t>=p, so ||b_p||^2<=integral_1^p 1/p^2 dt+integral_p^infinity t^-2 dt<=2/p, and dilation scales the squared norm by 1/m(n). This is an individual column bound, not a frame lower bound or a uniform bound on the inverse geometric Gram matrix.

## 4. Actual exact integer control
Executed JavaScript linear sieve and prime-tree transform through N=1,000,000 (78,498 primes). To avoid fractions use input a_n=n A_n with A_n=(n mod 29)-14. In scaled coordinates C_n=c_n/n the parent update becomes integer addition C_parent+=C_n; reconstruction gives A_n=C_n-sum_child C_child. Include index 1 with the coefficient corresponding to the condition P(1)=0. All 1,000,000 reconstruction checks passed, 967,839 nonzero edges, 999,999 total edges. Fixed signed 32-bit integer values in this example remain within range by sum |A_n|<=14N. This checks the finite coordinate transform, not any property of zeta zeros.

Executed core:
```js
const N=1000000, lp=new Int32Array(N+1), primes=[];
for(let n=2;n<=N;n++){if(lp[n]===0){lp[n]=n;primes.push(n);}for(const p of primes){if(p>lp[n]||n*p>N)break;lp[n*p]=p;}}
const a=new Int32Array(N+1),edge=new Int32Array(N+1),back=new Int32Array(N+1);
for(let n=2;n<=N;n++)a[n]=(n%29)-14;
const c=a.slice();let active=0;
for(let n=N;n>=2;n--){const m=n/lp[n];edge[n]=c[n];if(edge[n])active++;c[m]+=c[n];}
for(let n=2;n<=N;n++){const m=n/lp[n];back[n]+=edge[n];back[m]-=edge[n];}
for(let n=2;n<=N;n++)if(back[n]!==a[n])throw Error("coefficient mismatch "+n);
if(back[1]!==-a.reduce((x,y)=>x+y,0))throw Error("constant mismatch");
```

## 5. Remaining obstacle
All-prime tree corrections span exactly the same finite admissible geometric space as the earlier unrestricted matrix-free optimization, at each N. They remove the fixed-seed obstruction and the two-prime algebraic restriction; they do not improve the exact infimum just by changing coordinates. A uniform vanishing upper bound for that infimum is still missing. No independent RH proof or evidence of closeness to one is obtained by the finite basis theorem. The new basis can be used to investigate conditioning and constructive coefficient estimates, but the actual geometric norm estimate is the required theorem. Do not conflate coefficient-space stability or O(N) implementation with that theorem.
