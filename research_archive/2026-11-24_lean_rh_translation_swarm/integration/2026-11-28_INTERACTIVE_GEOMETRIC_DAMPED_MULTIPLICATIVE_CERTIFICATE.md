## 1. Arithmetic form of nonlinear descent
Use t=1/x and chi(t)=1_{t>=1}; r(t)=chi(t)+sum_{n=2}^N c_n b_n(t), with b_n(t)={t/n}-{t}/n. On integer cells its value is r_j=1+j A-sum c_n floor(j/n), A=sum c_n/n, and r_0=0. In Mellin variables R(s)=1-zeta(s)P(s), P(s)=sum c_n(n^(-s)-1/n), so Mellin(r)=R(s)/s. The jump coefficients d_j=r_j-r_(j-1) have Dirichlet series R(s) for Re s>1. Consequently the inverse Mellin transform of R(s)^2/s on a cell j is
 u_j=sum_{ab<=j}d_a d_b.
The fourth-moment approximation theorem in the preceding note implies that the finite admissible approximants to R^2 converge in the weighted L2 norm. On each fixed cell, the finite approximants converge to this u_j by the locally finite coefficient identity. Hence sum_{j<=L}u_j^2/[j(j+1)] is a lower bound on the full fourth moment. In fact norm convergence and cell convergence identify the entire inverse Mellin sequence. This computation evaluates only finite Dirichlet convolution, O(L log L) operations, not samples of zeta.

## 2. Finite admissible update with a dilation-tail enclosure
For any finite real polynomial B(s)=sum_{k<=M} a_k k^(-s), let P_new=P(1+B). Then P_new(1)=0 identically, and R_new=R+(R-1)B. This is a finite admissible witness with support <=NM. Its exact real arithmetic sequence is
 r_new(j)=r_j+sum_{k<=M} a_k (r_floor(j/k)-1_{j>=k}).
It is the damped multiplication step with a finite approximate residual B; no claim of approximating all frequencies at fixed M.

Let Q be the prior mean-square divisor energy of r and H(l)=Q[1/(l+1)+N^2/((l+1)(l+2))], a valid upper bound on its whole tail after l. In the t-coordinate the squared norm uses dt/t^2. For L>=M, triangle inequality and change of variables give
 sqrt(tail_new(L)) <= sqrt(H(L)) + sum_{k<=M}|a_k| sqrt(H(floor((L+1)/k)-1)/k) + |sum a_k|/sqrt(L+1).
The last term handles the constant part of chi(t/k) on the entire tail. The shifted integral begins at (L+1)/k>=floor((L+1)/k), explaining the floor. This enclosure uses the original N in H, not the enlarged support NM. It is rigorous for any finite coefficients.

## 5. Remaining proof
The nonlinear mechanism now has a certified finite descent instance, unlike the earlier purely conditional fourth-moment test. This does not supply a sequence with residual tending to zero: a hypothetical off-line zeta zero would impose a fixed positive lower bound on all admissible residuals, even after these updates. To establish RH still requires an independent descent theorem that cannot stall at positive residual. The damped update is useful diagnostically, not a mathematical completion. No further enlarging of numerical cutoffs alone resolves this obstacle.
