## 2. Construct the one orbit, maps, parity and residue field

Let Phi=Gal(E/K), identified with the infinite places, and put

    h = placeOfInt(-p),   d = order of h modulo N0,
    O = order(h),         c = h^d,   N = order(c),
    r = ceil(log_p m),    P0 = p^d.

Here P0 is the prime power called P in HeckeSizes, not the parameter
record P. Markedness with l in Lambda supplies: p is inert in K;
p<x1; d is positive and odd; d<=r; and m divides O.
Here is the order calculation in that served-prime lemma. Write
m=2^u, s=22u, q=-p; q^O=1 modulo 2^s. Since p is not congruent
to either sign modulo m, v2(q-1),v2(q+1)<u. An odd O would give
v2(q^O-1)=v2(q-1)<s, impossible. For O=2^j v with v odd,
factor q^O-1 into q^v-1, q^v+1 and the j-1 remaining terms
q^(2^i v)+1. Their valuations are respectively v2(q-1),
v2(q+1), and 1, since an odd square is 1 modulo 8. Thus
s<=v2(q^O-1)<=2u+j-3, giving j>=20u+3>=u.
Hence m divides O. This uses the exact IsServed premises;
it is not claimed for arbitrary nonsplit primes.

The image-order d divides O, so O=dN. Since m is a power of 2
and d is odd, m divides N. In particular N is positive and even;
write N=2 ell. Also N<=O<=|Phi|=f and
d<=phi(N0)<=N0<=c0. Inertness excludes every prime dividing N1*d_K,
where d_K is the quadratic discriminant parameter; consequently
p is unramified, is a uniformizer in W=O_{E,l}, and p>=13.

Choose the marked top tau inducing l, and define

    t_s = c^s tau,                 s in Z/N,
    v_s = [inducedPlace(t_s)=l].

The map s -> t_s is injective by order(c)=N. A place inducing l or
its conjugate differs from tau by a power h^i: the decomposition
group is generated by the Frobenius pair (h,conjugation). It is
a top precisely when d divides i. Thus this map is a bijection
onto the ACTUAL tops over l or its conjugate. Since d is odd,
t_{s+1}=placeOfInt(-p^d)t_s swaps those two primes. Hence
v_{s+1}=not(v_s), and translation by 1 bijects the even and odd
letters. Each has exactly ell elements. Therefore the actual
markExponent is

    A_l = a #even = a N/2.                              (1)

Define the ring map iota_s: W -> embValRing by restricting
Phi(t_s) when v_s is true and Phi(t_s) composed with conjugation
otherwise. In both cases the induced p-place is l, so elements
of W are integral and units remain units. The global unramified
Frobenius is F=(h,conjugation). As d is odd, h^d=F^d conjugation.
Toggling the chosen conjugation therefore gives, on O_E,

    iota_{s+1}(u) == iota_s(u)^(p^d) modulo p.            (2)

This is the p^d Frobenius congruence on the actual cyclotomic
extension, not just a congruence on its roots of unity. One can
check it on zeta and the integral quadratic generator and extend
across the order of p-prime index, as in frob_ext; equivalently
use unramified Frobenius on the whole local integer ring.
For u=a/b in W with b outside l, the images of b are units;
inverting its congruence extends (2) to all W. The quotient
embValRing/(p) need not be a field; only these units are inverted.

The residue cardinality at l is p^lcm(O,2). Since m is even and
m|O, this is p^O=(p^d)^N=P0^N. This checks the precise residue
field required by TwistingData without losing a factor 2.
Choose a complex square root w of p. Its p-adic norm squared is
|p|_p<1, so w belongs to embValRing and w^2=p there. Together
with p prime and P0>=13, the maps, parity, congruence and residue
calculation give EVERY field of the actual TwistingData.

## 3. Explicit scale consequences, before the guards

    C >= 10^12 c0,  L >= 100 C,
    H >= 10^6 L,    B >= 10^6 H,    6 log(H) <= C.

Since c0>=64, all of C,L,H,B exceed 100. In particular
B=exp(C), H<=exp(C/6), B>=10^12 L, and c0<=C.
The data intervals and rounding give

    exp(L/2) <= m <= 2 exp(L/2) <= exp(L),
    exp(4L) <= k <= exp(6L),
    exp(B+L) <= a <= exp(B+L+1).                         (3)

For clarity, the upper bound in the last line has no unspecified
rounding error. Write m=2^u and s=22u. Then
2^s=m^22 and 3^(floor(log_3 s)+5)<=243s<=7776L. Hence
N1<=7776 c0 L m^22<=7776*2^22 L^2 exp(11L)<=exp(12L).
The last inequality follows from

then differentiate). Thus roundUp(xb)<xb+N1<=e xb, giving
the a-bound in (3). Also f=phi(N1)<=N1<=exp(12L).
The k-bounds follow from k=r_data+1-m, the actual rank interval

## 4. Check every HeckeSizes conjunct

Use the native arguments

    (P,d,N,k,n,r,ell,kappa,y,z)
      = (p^d,d,N,k,a,r,N/2,x^(-14),x^40,
                         128 A_l B/(mp)).               (5)

The first five conjuncts are immediate from (2)--(4) and parity:
0<r, 2r<=k, kappa<=1, 1<=y, and ell+ell=N.
For 2r<=k, use 2e^L<=e^(4L)<=k.

For the sixth, the precise root/projector degree condition, (3)--(4)
give

    N a k(ell+2)+a+k
       <= 3e^(B+31L+1)+e^(B+L+1)+e^(6L)
       < e^(2B).

Here ell<=N, L>=100 and B>=10^12 L justify the final strict
inequality. On the other hand dN=O>=m, p>=13>e^2, so

    (p^d)^ell = p^(dN/2) > e^m >= e^(2B).

This proves the STRICT sixth guard, including all additive terms.

For the seventh guard put S=(k+k^2)dN^2. Bounds (3)--(4)
imply S<=2e^(37L). Consequently

    6(2S+2)+2 <= 24e^(37L)+14 <= e^(40L)=y.

For the eighth,

    2(N+2)k/y <= 6e^(-22L) <= e^(-14L)=kappa.

For the ninth we must control the ACTUAL P^(3r), rather than
replacing r by a large bound inside that power. Minimality of r
gives p^(r-1)<m, whence

    r log p < log m+log p <= L+H <= 2H.

Since d<=c0<=C<=e^(C/6) and H<=e^(C/6),

    log(P^(3r)) = 3dr log p <= 6e^(C/3) <= B/2.

Thus, keeping y=e^(40L),

    (y+N(k+1))kP^(3r)+k+2
        <= 3e^(B/2+46L)
        <= e^(B/2+48L)
        <= e^(B-39L) <= a/y.

The first inequality uses N(k+1)<=2e^(18L)<=y;
the third uses B>=174L, already far weaker than the actual
scale assumption. This checks the ninth guard with its original y.

For the last guard, p^r>=m and r>=1 imply
P^(2r)>=p^(2r)>=pm. Also
r^2 m<=e^(3L)<=k, so r^2/(kP)<=1/(mp).
Using (1),

    N a (P^(-2r)+r^2/(kP))
         <= 2 N a/(mp)
         <= 64 N a B/(mp) = 128 A_l B/(mp) = z.

No change to the native final constant is proposed. This supplies
all ten conjuncts, not a new asymptotic or an unchecked appeal to
the logarithmic-size tactic.
