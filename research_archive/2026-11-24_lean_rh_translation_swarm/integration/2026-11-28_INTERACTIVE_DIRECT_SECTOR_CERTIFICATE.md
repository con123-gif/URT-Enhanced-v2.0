## 1. A direct replacement for the two moment inputs (conditional)
Let W=D.rows, d=dim W>0, v=dim D.Column>0. Row positivity is a separate obligation in this route (do not use original spread to establish it). Let V_s=range of the actual projected ramified column map on W. All maps use the same admissible data, same ramifying operator and same scalar normalization as A. No change to the arithmetic index or shortness assumptions. Let P_s be the orthogonal projection onto V_s, and Qbar=E_s P_s. In this section set a=v/(4d) and sigma=b^(-3/2). Suppose
    dim spectral subspace of Qbar on [a,1] >= (1-sigma/2)d.
This is a NEW SUFFICIENT HYPOTHESIS, not claimed proved. It replaces both the near-isometry and second-moment bounds in the terminal filling argument; it does not claim their arithmetic suppliers are absent in any future proof of this hypothesis. Because sigma<1 and d>0, the good subspace W+ is nonzero; since Qbar<=I, a<=1 and v<=4d. If the native good projector acts on a zero target, this hypothesis alone is vacuous: retain d>0 explicitly.

For any currently selected sum F of V_s let J project onto W+ intersect F^perp. Put r=dim(W+ intersect F^perp). Then tr(J Qbar)>=ar, because J has range in W+. Some s satisfies rank(J P_s)>=tr(J P_s)>=ar (trace is squared HS norm of JP_s, whose singular values are at most 1). To get a simple recurrence, use instead F+ = orthogonal projection of F onto W+, and r=dim W+ - dim F+. Its missing subspace in W+ is W+ intersect F^perp as above. Adding V_s raises dim F+ by rank(J P_s), hence r_next<=(1-a)r. While r>sigma*d/2, the selected translate is new (positive rank gain). Thus at most
    j=ceil[(4d/v) log(2/sigma)]
translates suffice, with r<=sigma*d/2 (using 1-a<=exp(-a)). The original unprojected F then has dim F>=dim F+ >=(1-sigma)d. Its cost is
    |t| v <= 4d log(2/sigma)+v <= d(6 log b+4 log 2+4)
           <= 5d log(1+b²)
for b>=1000. Last step follows from 4log2+4<=4log b and log(1+b²)>=2log b. Extract independent rows from the SAME A family using ShortAndEnough/LongForms. Ramification is incorporated in V_s from the outset; no error for restricting after selection. Hence this spectral counting hypothesis yields almost-full rank at the original translate budget without evaluating the upper spectrum or any moment. More generally sigma=b^(-1-delta), fixed 0<delta<1, works asymptotically: selection cost <=d[4(1+delta)log b+4log2+4]<5d log(1+b²) eventually.

## 2. Keep the actual compact completion cost, not its rounded bound

Here I is the index budget, V=n h with h=H-mR+L. Justification: compact CrudeRows.lean (e84f4b0e67ed7dbfd727f8ec2dbf05a8d1e5440c) supplies a spanning family with all coordinate squared norms <=exp(x1²b²) and nonzero gamma costing 2m² squares; MonomialCoefficientVector.lean Data.normSqAt_goodProjTranslate_le adds x^99 to the logarithmic norm after projection; CostOfSquares.lean defines per-square reciprocal height 2t x^999; OneDeterminant.lean (329f004f845b9e2becc896419c8594e82dfef0a6) adds k*g for k=d-n.     d(H-2L)<=n h+2I+(d-n)U.
Rearrange exactly (no sign assumption about h):

Since native -h<=b² x^99, for sigma=b^(-3/2) it suffices to bound sigma(U+b²x^99)<=L. ## 3. How to target the new certificate directly
At the actual native caller, V_s=U_s V_1 with unitary U_s and central good projector (scalar normalization irrelevant to ranges, ramifyingOp fixed before s); use the actual coordinate identifications in FillingTheorem.lean. Thus Qbar is the group twirl of P_1, not the unnormalized Poincare energy. For an orthogonal unitary isotypic decomposition W=direct sum over lambda of V_lambda tensor M_lambda, let d_lambda=dim V_lambda. By Schur orthogonality,
    Qbar|lambda = I_{V_lambda} tensor A_lambda,
    A_lambda = (1/d_lambda) Tr_{V_lambda}(E_lambda P_1 E_lambda).
Here E_lambda is the isotypic projection, partial trace over the irreducible factor. Proof: average annihilates inter-isotypic maps and commutes with the action; partial trace in each block is invariant under conjugation on V_lambda and gives the displayed factor. The new target is exactly
    sum_lambda d_lambda * # {eigenvalues of A_lambda < v/(4d)}
       <= (sigma/2)d.
This can alternatively be proved by finding for each sector a subspace of multiplicity space of dimension k_lambda where A_lambda's quadratic form is >=a, and summing weighted k_lambda (min-max). No control of large eigenvalues is necessary, nor is a uniform lower bound on EVERY sector necessary. The original group can be a product, but multiplicities must not be dropped. This is the precise proposed arithmetic task, not a computational measurement or a claim of novelty.

Separation from a near-tight moment: abstract example with d=2v (even), split W into two v-dimensional invariant sectors, Qbar eigenvalues 1/4 and 3/4 with equal multiplicities, e.g. cyclic permutation within each sector twirling a coordinate projection selecting v/4 and 3v/4 coordinates (v divisible by 4). This is the twirl of an actual rank-v orthogonal projection; trace=v; every eigenvalue >=v/(4d)=1/8, so the direct certificate holds even with sigma=0. But tr(Qbar²)/(v²/d)=5/4, a fixed 25% relative error, violating any b^-1-delta near-tight target for large b. With T isometric onto V_1, M=Qbar; this is a concrete strict separation, not just a new parameterization of the same moment bound. Conversely if V_1 lies in a proper invariant subspace, the corresponding missing sectors have eigenvalue zero; symmetry alone cannot establish the new target.
