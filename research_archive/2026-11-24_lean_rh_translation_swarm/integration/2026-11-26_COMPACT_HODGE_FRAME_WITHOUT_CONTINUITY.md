## 1. The two classical scalar facts, with exact hypotheses

Let G=Gal(Q_p-bar/Q_p), C=C_p and χ the cyclotomic character. For every
OPEN subgroup H=G_K of G, K is a finite extension of Q_p and

    C^H=K,              C(q)^H=0 if q∈Z is nonzero.     (T)

The action on C(q) is g·x=χ(g)^q g(x). Thus if an equality
χ(g)^u g(x)=χ(g)^v x, u≠v, holds on a neighborhood of 1, choose
an open subgroup inside that neighborhood and use (T) with q=u−v
to obtain x=0. If g(x)=x on a neighborhood, (T) with q=0 places
x in the corresponding finite K, hence in Q_p-bar.

These are Tate's invariant theorem and the zero-twist Ax–Sen–Tate
case. Primary statement with the open-subgroup scope: Fontaine,
*Le corps des périodes p-adiques*, Astérisque 223 (1994), §1.5.7,
printed p.74, following gr^q(B_dR)=C(q) in §1.5.5; that section
cites Tate's 1967 Theorems 1 and 2. The primary theorem text was
opened again in this session:
[Fontaine's paper](https://www.numdam.org/item/AST_1994__223__59_0.pdf).
The source NearOne also proves (T) directly, from normalized traces
and Ax; no Hodge frame or continuity of the representation is used.

The statements apply to the NATURAL Galois action on C and χ. They
do not assume that the unrelated matrix representation below is
continuous. This distinction is what removes the circularity.

## 2. A stable subspace is generated by normalized algebraic vectors

Let I be finite, u_i∈Z, and let W⊂C^I be a C-linear subspace.
Let j:Γ→G have open image. More generally allow a filter F on Γ
such that nhds_G(1)≤j_*F. Suppose, for every v∈W,

    T_γ(v):=(χ(jγ)^(u_i) jγ(v_i))_i∈W
                                     for F-almost all γ.       (S)

Then W is spanned over C by vectors a having all the following
properties, with the SAME chosen coordinate ν:

- a_i∈Q_p-bar and |a_i|≤1;
- a_ν=1;
- a_i=0 whenever u_i≠u_ν;
- jγ(a_i)=a_i for F-almost all γ, for each i.

Proof. First choose a nonzero vector v of minimal support among
vectors of W with a prescribed finite support bound. Every vector
of W supported within supp(v) is proportional to v. Otherwise
eliminate a nonzero coordinate between two nonproportional vectors;
the resulting nonzero vector has smaller support, a contradiction.
Choose ν with |v_ν| maximal and put a=v/v_ν. Then |a_i|≤1 and
a_ν=1. For F-almost all γ, (S) puts T_γ(a) in W, and its support
is contained in supp(a), so it is proportional to a. Its ν-coordinate
is χ(jγ)^(u_ν). Therefore, for every i,

    χ(jγ)^(u_i) jγ(a_i)=χ(jγ)^(u_ν) a_i.               (4)

Since I is finite these equations may be imposed on one F-large
set. The predicate in (4) depends only on jγ, so nhds_G(1)≤j_*F
makes it true on a Galois neighborhood. The unequal-weight case
of (T) gives a_i=0 for u_i≠u_ν. In the equal-weight case, (4)
itself gives F-almost fixedness, and its neighborhood consequence
with the zero-weight case of (T) gives a_i∈Q_p-bar. This proves
all properties, including their original F quantifiers.

Finally, these minimal-support vectors span all W, not just its
lines of minimal support. For any v∈W choose such a vector a with
support contained in supp(v); at its normalized coordinate ν,
v−v_νa has strictly smaller support. Induct on that finite support.
This is the elementary elimination behind le_of_echelon and
le_of_hodgeTate_stable. No topological closure or infinite sum is used.

## 3. Exact Hodge-frame statement

Put D=B_dR^+, with uniformizer t satisfying γ(t)=χ(jγ)t, and let
A∈GL_d(B_dR). Let N(γ) be matrices over Q_p-bar such that

    N(γ) jγ(A)=A                  for F-almost all γ.    (5)

Assume the same filter hypothesis nhds_G(1)≤j_*F. Then there are

    Y∈SL_d(O_(Q_p-bar)),  P∈GL_d(D),  k_i∈Z

with jγ(Y)=Y for F-almost all γ, such that

    A Y=P diag(t^(k_i)).                               (6)

Here O_(Q_p-bar) means algebraic p-adic integers, so every entry
of Y has norm at most 1; its inverse is integral too. This is the
actual conclusion required from exists_hodge_frame. It makes no
continuity assumption on N(γ).

## 4. Minimal determinant and one-column improvement

Consider all factorizations A Y=P diag(t^k) with Y as in section 3,
P merely integral over D and det P≠0. Such a factorization exists:
take Y=I, choose n≥0 clearing all t-poles of A, and use P=t^n A,
k_i=−n. Among these candidates minimize ord_t(det P), a nonnegative
integer. Only this discrete valuation is minimized; no limiting
choice of a matrix is made.

Suppose the reduction Pbar over C were singular, and put
W=ker(Pbar). From (5),(6) and F-almost fixedness of Y,

    N(γ) jγ(P) diag(χ(jγ)^(k_i))=P.

Taking the residue gives
N(γ) jγ(Pbar) diag(χ^(k_i))=Pbar. Therefore W is stable under

    a↦(χ(jγ)^(−k_i) jγ(a_i))_i.                       (7)

The MINUS sign here matters: substitute (7) into Pbar and cancel
the two diagonal characters. Apply section 2 with u_i=−k_i to
the nonzero kernel. It provides a normalized algebraic integer
vector a∈W with a_ν=1, a_i=0 for k_i≠k_ν, and F-almost fixed.

Let E be the identity matrix with column ν replaced by a. Its
determinant is a_ν=1, its entries are algebraic integers, and it is
F-almost fixed. Also E commutes with diag(t^k): the only changed
column is supported where k_i=k_ν. The column P a has residue
Pbar a=0, so every entry is divisible by t in D. Divide JUST that
column by t to define an integral matrix P', and put k'=k+e_ν.
Then

    A(YE)=P' diag(t^(k'_i)),
    det P'=det P/t.                                   (8)

YE is another permitted determinant-one integral algebraic matrix,
fixed on the intersection of the finitely many F-large sets. Thus
(8) is a candidate with smaller determinant order, contradicting
minimality. Pbar is consequently invertible, and so is P over D.
This proves (6). For d=0 the empty matrices already give the result.

## 5. Jumps and the initial crystalline period matrix

Apply section 3 to an invariant crystalline period basis matrix M,
using F=top (all γ) at the actual IsCrystalline caller. Its image
under j is an OPEN subgroup, so nhds_G(1)≤j_*top. We obtain Y
fixed by every γ and hence with entries in the actual finite
local fixed field K. The same is true for its inverse.

Audit RUN61 makes the native dual-functional dictionary explicit.
Put R=theta(P). Its inverse rows f_i satisfy
f_i(N(g)v)=chi(g)^(-k_i) g(f_i(v)) on the rational lattice basis.
For a weight-u functional sum_i c_i f_i, comparison gives
c_i=chi(g)^(k_i-u) g(c_i). Tate forces c_i=0 for k_i!=u;
otherwise c_i is algebraic. Conversely equal-weight algebraic
combinations satisfy the required relation on a common open subgroup.
Thus the multiplicity of u is #{i:k_i=u} OVER Qpbar, not a claimed
C_p-dimension of all eventually invariant functions. The native
hasJumps_of_rows uses these inverse rows; no replacement by the
columns of R or opposite sign is made.

Every φ^n M is again an invariant basis since φ commutes with
Galois action and fixes the Z_p representation matrices. Apply
the same result to each of them. Their jumps are intrinsic to the
same representation. Thus the actual hypothesis that all jumps
lie in [−a,b] bounds every resulting k_i(n) in that interval.
The entrywise pole bounds for φ^n M and its inverse now hold for
ALL n≥0, as required by the pole-removal criterion.

The already derived fixed-coefficient appendix constructs

    U=(Eτ)^a M,       U'=(Eτ)^b M^(-1) over B+,
    UU'=(Eτ)^(a+b)I,
    φU=v^a U F,       F,F^(-1) over S_B,

and the integral Galois transform. Section 3 of that appendix proves
preservation of every fixed-field linear-form depth. Using the
Hodge frame just supplied, it proves the full hat_stable field:
for EVERY y over B+, if Uy∈E^(a+b)B+^d then
U fhat(y)∈E^(a+b)B+^d. The witness on the right of each
divisibility is over B+, not required to be integral over A_inf.

Consequently the general Hodge frame no longer needs a continuity
assumption proved by itself. The chain is scalar Tate invariants →
support elimination → minimal determinant → Hodge frame → period
matrix → integral basis/frame/radius. Automatic continuity may
then be derived downstream, as in the native source. This is an
acyclic supplier chain from the stated scalar theorems and period-ring
construction. It is not an independent audit of every earlier supplier.

## 6. Correct height interface at the actual frame consumer

Let a=L.jumpBound. FrameTheorem calls the initial construction with
lower and upper bounds BOTH a. Its period height is therefore

    H=2a,      QL=τ^(2a)I, UZ=E^(2a)Q, Z'Z=E^(2a)I.

Define its actual frame vectors by τ^a n_j=s(Qe_j), not τ^H n_j.
For an integral original vector x=s(α),

    τ^a ν_0(Lα)=s(QLα)=τ^(2a)x,
    hence ν_0(Lα)=τ^a x.

The coordinate test extracts E_m^(2a)t_j, namely E_m^H t_j,
not E_m^(2H)t_j. This gives the original τ-saturation induction.
For the radius lemma retain abstract height H in Z'Z=E^H I,
but choose its INDEPENDENT normalization parameter b=a. Thus
φ^lZ=E_l^a C V cancels E_l^a in the form evaluation. The
result is exactly the already derived p^(4δ) span. Audit eec17037
corrects the earlier human conflation; it is incorporated here
