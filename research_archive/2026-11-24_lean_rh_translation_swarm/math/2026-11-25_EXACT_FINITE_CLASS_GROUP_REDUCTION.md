# Count I: exact finite class-group reduction, with local conditions

Let K=Q(zeta_(2^(E+2)))^+(sqrt(-q),sqrt(-d)), D=[K:Q]=2^(E+2), N=Dqd. q,d are distinct odd primes in the actual host. Let l be odd, j>=1, and assume l-1 does not divide 2j (the numerical condition in CaseI). Set M=Q_l/Z_l(-j), A_m=M[l^m], F_m=K(mu_(l^m)), Gamma_m=Gal(F_m/K). Denote S(K,j) the strict absolute H1 kernel (inertia zero away from l, decomposition zero at l); equivalent to source Sha with compatible places. Let C_m=Cl(F_m)/<classes of primes above l>.

**Exact result:** S(K,j)[l^m] ≅ Hom(C_m,A_m)^Gamma_m, for every m>=1, where action on Hom is sigma.f(c)=sigma(f(sigma^(-1)c)) (cyclotomic inverse j twist on values).

Proof:
1. K_v/Q_l has ramification index at most 2 for every v|l: the dyadic real tower is unramified at odd primes; of the two independent quadratic fields at most one has ramification at l, since q,d distinct and their odd discriminants are -q,-d. The intersection of K_v with Q_l(mu_l) is of degree at most 2 because the latter extension is totally ramified. Thus the mod-l local cyclotomic image contains the squares in F_l^*. Since l-1 does not divide 2j, some square has inverse j-th power different from 1. Its action minus identity is a unit on M (and on A_m), so both global invariants M^G_K and local invariants at l vanish.
2. The coefficient exact sequence 0->A_m->M --l^m->M->0 identifies H1(K,A_m) with H1(K,M)[l^m], and likewise at every v|l, since the global/local H0 terms vanish. At inertia outside l, action on both coefficients is trivial, so H1(I,A_m)->H1(I,M) is injective (Hom inclusion). Therefore strict local conditions for the torsion classes are preserved exactly.
3. F_m contains mu_(l^m), so G_Fm acts trivially on A_m. Gamma_m is abelian; its local decomposition subgroup at any v|l is abelian too. Each contains an element with action minus identity invertible on A_m, by step 1. Cohomology H^i(Gamma_m,A_m)=0 for all i>0 and likewise for the local subgroup: for an abelian group a central element acts trivially on cohomology via inner conjugation but acts via its scalar action on coefficients, hence scalar-minus-one annihilates cohomology and is invertible. Alternatively average over the prime-to-l subgroup containing the tame scalar. Inflation-restriction now gives H1(K,A_m) ≅ Hom_cont(G_Fm,A_m)^Gamma_m and injectivity of restriction at all v|l.
4. F_m/K is unramified outside l, so inertia groups outside l coincide in the common algebraic closure. A homomorphism on G_Fm satisfying the strict conditions kills all finite inertia and all decomposition groups at l; F_m totally complex. Class field theory identifies these homomorphisms with Hom(C_m,A_m). Conversely every equivariant such homomorphism lifts by step 3 and is strict by the inertia identity and local injectivity. This proves the claimed isomorphism. It is equivariant for automorphisms of K/Q using the natural coefficient action; projection to the source plus sector requires its precise complex-conjugation convention. The total bound automatically bounds either summand.

Quantitative consequence: at m=1 the residual rank r_l=dim_Fl S(K,j)[l] satisfies
 r_l log l <= log h(F_1) <= D(l-1) log(N*l*zeta(2)).
More generally log |S(K,j)[l^m]| <= log h(F_m) <= D*phi(l^m) log(N*l^m*zeta(2)).
Proof of second inequality: degree d_m<=D*phi(l^m), conductor divides N*l^m (not necessarily equals it); conductor-discriminant gives |disc(F_m)|<=(N*l^m)^d_m. Minkowski gives integral ideal representative norm <=sqrt(|disc(F_m)|) (usual prefactor <=1). Number of integral ideals of norm <=X is <=X^2*zeta_Fm(2)<=X^2*zeta(2)^d_m by Euler factors. Also |Hom(C_m,A_m)|<=|C_m|<=h(F_m). These are unconditional human bounds, not a sharp estimate. The exact identity means one may seek a bound directly on equivariant class group components, rather than recomputing cohomology at every level.

Limitation: RHS grows at least like the presented degree bound l^(m-1) as m increases; cannot take m->infinity to obtain a uniform length estimate. Even the m=1 estimate grows with l and does not sum over arbitrary finite coefficient-prime sets. Washington (Invent Math 49, 1978, pp 87-97) gives effective fixed-base fixed-prime stabilization for class number along dyadic tower, not a bound that is uniform in l,m,q,d.
