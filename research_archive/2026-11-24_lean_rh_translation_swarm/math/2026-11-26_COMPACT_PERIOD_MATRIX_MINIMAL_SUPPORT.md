## Exact theorem and quantifiers

Let `R` be a commutative valuation domain, `d` a finite type, and `M : Matrix d d R`. The native theorem says

\[
  \det M=0\Longrightarrow
  \exists a:d\to R\;\exists \nu:d,\quad
  a_\nu=1,\quad Ma=0,
\]

and, for **every** ring endomorphism `g : R →+* R`,

\[
  M(g\circ a)=0\Longrightarrow g\circ a=a. \tag{\*}
\]

The last implication is conditional: the theorem does not assert that an arbitrary endomorphism preserves the kernel. The caller separately proves that premise through the transformed period-matrix identities.

## Minimal derivation

### 1. Minimal support and normalization

Because `det M = 0`, choose a nonzero `w` in `ker M`. Among nonzero kernel vectors choose one whose support

\[
  \operatorname{supp}(w)=\{i:w_i\ne0\}
\]

has minimal cardinality. A valuation ring totally orders the principal ideals generated by the finitely many nonzero coordinates, so one coordinate `t=w_ν` divides every `w_i`. Write

\[
  w=t a,\qquad a_\nu=1. \tag{1}
\]

The domain hypothesis and `t ≠ 0` cancel `t` from `M(ta)=0`, hence `Ma=0`. This is the point of the valuation-ring assumption: over a general domain there need not be one coordinate dividing all the others.

### 2. Fixedness from exact support loss

Let `g : R →+* R` satisfy `M(g∘a)=0`. Then

\[
  b=(g\circ a)-a\in\ker M. \tag{2}
\]

If `a_i=0`, then `g(a_i)-a_i=0`, so `supp(b) ⊆ supp(a)`. At the normalized coordinate,

\[
  b_\nu=g(a_\nu)-a_\nu=g(1)-1=0. \tag{3}

Thus a nonzero `b` would have strictly smaller support than `a`, contradicting the choice of `w=ta`; scalar multiplication by nonzero `t` does not change support. Hence `b=0`, proving (\*).

The decisive equation for this local obstruction is therefore simply

\[
  \boxed{\ b_\nu=g(1)-1=0,\qquad
  \operatorname{supp}(b)\subsetneq\operatorname{supp}(a)\quad(b\ne0)\ }. \tag{4}
\]

It converts normalization into a strict-support contradiction; no norm estimate, fitted constant, or RH premise enters.

## Exact finite certificate

An exhaustive independent implementation used `GF(4)=GF(2)[x]/(x^2+x+1)`. For every square matrix of dimensions 2 and 3 it:

1. enumerated every nonzero kernel vector;
2. selected a vector of minimum support;
3. normalized its first nonzero coordinate to 1;
4. applied Frobenius `g(x)=x^2`;
5. checked `M g(a)=0 → g(a)=a`.

| dimension | all matrices | singular matrices | cases with `M g(a)=0` | verified |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 256 | 76 | 46 | 46 |
| 3 | 262,144 | 80,704 | 27,406 | 27,406 |

Negative controls:

- Minimality removed: for the zero `2×2` matrix and `a=(1,α)`, Frobenius gives `(1,α+1)≠a`, while both vectors remain in the kernel.
- Normalization removed: for `M=diag(0,1)` and `w=(α,0)`, Frobenius gives `(α+1,0)≠w`; normalizing gives `(1,0)`, which is fixed.

These are diagnostics, not a substitute for the general proof. They show that both hypotheses isolated in (1)–(3) are necessary for this proof strategy.
