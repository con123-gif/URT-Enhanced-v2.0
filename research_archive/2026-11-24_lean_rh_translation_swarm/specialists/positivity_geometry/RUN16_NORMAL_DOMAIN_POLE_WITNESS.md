## 1. Exact lemma and maximal colon ideal
GlobalTheory.ContinuousQuotient.exists_prime_of_not_dvd: Let A be a commutative Noetherian integrally closed domain; a,b in A, b nonzero, b does not divide a. Then there exist a prime ideal p and s,r in A such that s is outside p, r is in p, and b s=a r. This says a/b has a genuine algebraic pole at p. Do not replace the hypothesis by a field assumption, and do not assume UFD.

Put I=(b), J(x)=(I:x)={t : b divides tx}. By Noetherianity choose a maximal member J(c) among all J(x) with b not dividing x and J(a) contained in J(x). The set is nonempty because x=a qualifies. Set p=J(c). Since b does not divide c, 1 is not in p; b belongs to p. If uv belongs to p but u does not, then b does not divide uc and J(c) is contained in J(uc): b|tc implies b|tuc. Maximality forces equality. Now v belongs to J(uc) because b|uvc, hence v belongs to p. Thus p is prime. Also J(a) is contained in p; in particular b|ta implies t belongs to p. This latter condition is the exact reason the reverse valuation branch is impossible.

## 2. Determinant trick constructs a principal localization
Claim: there are q in p and w outside p with b w=q c. Suppose not. For each q in p, b divides qc, so there exists w in A with b w=q c. By the negation of the claim every such w belongs to p. Embed A in its fraction field K and set y=c/b, N=image(p) in K. Then yN is contained in N. N is nonzero because b is in p and b is nonzero, and finitely generated by Noetherianity. This is exactly the source application of isIntegral_of_smul_mem_submodule.

Readable determinant argument: choose generators n_1,...,n_d of N; d>=1, some n_i nonzero. Write y n_i=sum_j M_ij n_j with M_ij in A. Over K, (y I-M)n=0. Multiply by the adjugate to get det(y I-M)n=0. Since K is a field and some n_i is nonzero, det(y I-M)=0. Thus y is a root of a monic polynomial in A[T]. Integral closedness forces y in A, i.e. b divides c, contradiction. The source does not posit a principal global ideal p or assume its height. It obtains a special pair q,w.

Now R=A_p, a Noetherian local domain, A injects into R. For x in p write b v=x c. From b w=q c we have b w x=b v q, so w x=v q by cancellation of b. Since w is a unit in R, x=(v/w)q there. Conversely q is in p. Thus the maximal ideal pR=(q) is principal. It is nonzero because b survives in it, so R is not a field. The standard DVR equivalence for a Noetherian local domain with nonzero principal maximal ideal gives a valuation ring. ## 3. Two-way divisibility and the exact witness
In R the images of a and b are comparable by divisibility. Write the resulting multiplier as r/s, where s lies outside p. There are two possibilities.
(1) b=a(r/s). Clearing the denominator and using A→R injective gives b s=a r in A. Then b divides ar and J(a) is contained in p, so r is in p. This is the desired witness.
(2) a=b(r/s). Clearing gives a s=b r, so s belongs to J(a) and hence p. This contradicts the localization denominator s outside p.
The result is stronger than a bare assertion of height-one obstruction: it supplies the actual numerator-denominator relation in A. As an explanatory consequence p has height one because A_p is a DVR; the original theorem statement only requests primality and the witness.

Normality is essential. In A=C[t²,t³], b=t² and a=t³ have a/b=t outside A. Any b s=a r forces s=t r in C[t]. If r is in A and t r is in A, r has zero constant coefficient. More strongly, s²=t²r²=b r², so for ANY prime containing r, s is also in that prime. Thus no witness as in the theorem exists. The nonzero ideal (t²,t³) is preserved by multiplication by t: t·t²=t³, t·t³=t²·t². The determinant trick correctly gives integrality (T²-t²), but without normality integrality does not imply membership in A. This is an exact counterexample to deleting the hypothesis.
