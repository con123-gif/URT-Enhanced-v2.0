## 1. Exact name expansion, with only the permitted denominator

Sources: New/Curve/IntegralVacuum.lean c34fe7625b03009fbd5954ea00209b425ca603b1; isAdmissibleComb_name 19–39; New/Seesaw/GoodDatumExists.lean cc6f93272fb65dd22d8079236a66454b034182b9 49–55; New/Exchange/CrudeModularData.lean 2be49b2690280e070d21691f8a569fc81e113b2b 64–70; New/Seesaw/GradingsOfY.lean 9a0ce95531f7ef503b23167e9d90ef3d417f1bf8 35–38, 191–222.

Let R=O_E/m be the residue ring of the actual modulus, I the coordinate set of size h, and F=R^I. Let psi be its native primitive additive character. For any function Psi on F, character separation gives the exact identity
    Psi(x)=sum_{a in F} Psi(a) (1/|F|) sum_{b in F} psi((x-a) dot b).
Indeed, if x!=a, some coordinate d is nonzero. Primitivity (no nonzero ideal in the kernel) yields a b0 with psi(d*b0)!=1; translating the inner sum by b0 forces it to vanish. If x=a, every term is one. This works over the residue ring, without requiring a field. The actual name_char_eq_translate_projOn changes each character name into a finite Heisenberg translate of v=P^m u, multiplied by a finite character phase. Its translation uses an integral lift of -b and the local reciprocal modulus generator at the naming places; retain the exact phase from IntegralVacuum 32–37, not a phase-free assertion. The lift property gives support in the weighted translation group with weight conjugate(d), where d*q reduces to zero in R. At w not above q, q is a unit, so d divided by the local modulus generator is integral there; after conjugation the same holds for the translated coordinate. At places outside the naming support the coordinate is zero. These are the actual support conditions of isAdmissibleComb_name, using LatticeAndTorsionDefs 44–52.

Suppose g*q belongs to m, with g the algebraic integer supplied in the caller, and q a positive rational integer. For g nonzero, |R| divides the absolute norm of (gq), since (gq) is contained in m. Thus
    |F|=|R|^h divides N((g))^h q^([E:Q]*h).
For any rational prime l coprime to q, the coefficient N((g))^h/|F| is l-adically integral (cancel the power of q, an l-adic unit). Every psi value and every Heisenberg phase is a root of unity, hence integral and of absolute value one after every complex field automorphism. Therefore the actual Fourier expansion has admissible denominator N((g))^h and conjugate l1 bound sum_a |sigma(Psi(a))|: the |F| terms each have absolute size |sigma(Psi(a))|/|F|. Grouping repeated translates only improves the bound by the triangle inequality. This is the native finite Fourier supplier, not harmonic truncation.

## 3. Determinant-character factor: two genuinely distinct norm guards

Sources: Lib/ClassField/HeckeCharacter/CharacterConjugates.lean aeadd310d540e48ae4c0ce8d129330d6c413cc66 112–143; New/Seesaw/IntegralAway.lean 26–36; GoodDatumExists 65–66 and 82.     mu(u)^M = product_tau Phi_tau(gamma)^e_tau
and u^M*gamma has unit local norm at every finite place. For any complex automorphism sigma, sigma composed with Phi_tau is another complex embedding of the CM field, and all conjugates of gamma have norm 1 because complex conjugation agrees with the CM involution. Taking the Mth root of a positive real number gives |sigma(mu(u))|=1, including inverse factors.

For any embedding iota into C_l, take norms of the same identity. The equality |u_tau|_l^M * |iota(Phi_tau(gamma))|_l=1 gives
    |iota(mu(u))|_l = product_tau |u_tau|_l^(-e_tau).
If l is coprime to q, each place above l does not divide q, so hy makes the determinant of every stage point a local unit at each such place. Every factor is 1. This establishes the l-adic unit guard at 65–66 and the complex conjugate guard at 82, for the actual product of determinant characters and their inverses in liftWeilFin.
