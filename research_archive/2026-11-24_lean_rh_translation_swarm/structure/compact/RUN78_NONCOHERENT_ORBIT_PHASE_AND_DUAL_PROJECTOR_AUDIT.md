## 1. The exact quotient phase cancellation (a smaller sufficient lemma)

Source Lib/Adelic/QuotientMeasure/CocompactIntegrable.lean 2e4fa030a20738c99389a926add53ef1a2ddc5a2, lines 41–49 and 145–177. The actual caller only requires the integrable case of integral_quotMeasure_eq_zero_of_twist.

Let G be the second countable locally compact Hausdorff group of the source, mu left Haar, Gamma a discrete subgroup, F:G→V a continuous function into the finite-dimensional complex kernel space (the proof also works in the native complete complex normed space). Assume F is left Gamma-invariant and integrable on Gamma\G for its counting-normalized quotient measure. Suppose F(u y)=c F(y) for all y, |c|=1, c≠1. No assumption that u normalizes Gamma.

If F=0 there is nothing to prove. Otherwise fix y0 with F(y0)≠0 and a continuous complex-linear functional ell with ell(F(y0))=1. Let H be the closed subgroup of h in G such that F(h y)=chi(h) F(y) for every y, with |chi(h)|=1. This is a subgroup: the scalar is unique, multiplication multiplies scalars, and the inverse inverts it. It is closed because the scalar must be ell(F(h y0)), and all defining equalities and the unit-circle condition are closed. Thus H is locally compact, contains Gamma and u, and chi:H→U(1) is a continuous character trivial on Gamma. In particular the smaller closure of the group generated by Gamma and u would also work.

Use precisely the closed-subgroup Haar disintegration used in the source's integral_quotMeasure_eq_zero_of_mean: a measurable transverse parameter x with measure m and left Haar dh on H such that (x,h)↦hx pushes m×dh to mu (x denotes its selected representative). Apply integral_quotMeasure_stages to Gamma as subgroup of H. This retained standard quotient-Haar input yields

    J(x)=integral_{Gamma\H} F(hx) dnu(h)
with integrable slices almost everywhere, where nu is the counting-normalized quotient of dh. Apply the same stages formula to the norm to obtain absolute integrability of almost all slices. No G-invariant measure on H\G is presumed when H is nonunimodular.

On each such slice, ||F(hx)||=||F(x)||. If F(x)=0 the slice is zero. Otherwise absolute integrability implies 0<nu(Gamma\H)<∞ (positivity follows from quotient Haar normalization on a nonempty locally injective open set). Right multiplication [h]↦[hu] is a well-defined bijection of the LEFT quotient Gamma\H, whether or not u normalizes Gamma. It scales nu by a positive constant: this follows from the right-translation modulus of left Haar dh and uniqueness of the quotient measure in Weil's counting-unfolding formula. Because nu has finite nonzero total mass, that constant is 1. Consequently

Since c≠1, J(x)=0. Integrating gives I=0. If starting with the source's unconditional Bochner-integral convention, the nonintegrable case is zero by definition; the actual CrudeEnergy application has integrability and does not use this convention as a convergence argument.

This gives a direct eigencharacter proof of the unit-phase specialization, without the general stabilizer-of-distance function and probability mean construction of lines 148–177. It retains the actual Haar stages/disintegration hypothesis; it is not a claim that left multiplication by u descends to Gamma\G. No finite approximation is involved.
