## Exact statement and conventions

Let r=product_i p_i^(a_i), where the p_i are distinct primes and the a_i are
integer multiplicities at least 2. With ell the number of these primes,
prove

product_i p_i^(a_i^2/4) <= r^(log_2(r)/4 + 1 - ell)

unless r is one of 36, 72, 900. The displayed fractional exponents and
logarithm are real quantities. Integer division is not intended. The
argument below also proves strict failure at each of the three listed
exceptions. It works for positive ell and for the empty-product convention
ell=0, r=1, where both sides are 1.

## Logarithmic reduction

Set s_i=log_2(p_i), so s_i>=1. Put

S=sum_i s_i, x_i=a_i-2>=0, X=sum_i x_i*s_i,
L=sum_i a_i*s_i=log_2(r)=2S+X.

Taking base-2 logarithms, the proposed inequality is exactly D>=0, where

D=L^2-sum_i a_i^2*s_i-4(ell-1)L.

Expansion gives the exact cancellation

D = 4S(S-2ell+1) + 4(S-ell)X + H,
H = X^2-sum_i x_i^2*s_i >= 0.

To prove H>=0, write z_i=x_i*s_i>=0. Then
sum_i x_i^2*s_i <= sum_i z_i^2 <= (sum_i z_i)^2=X^2.
The first inequality follows from s_i>=1, and the second by expanding
the square and retaining its nonnegative cross terms. No approximation
or finite search occurs in this reduction.

## Elementary logarithmic lower bounds

For a prime p,

- p>=2 gives log_2(p)>=1;
- p>=3 gives log_2(p)>=3/2, since 3^2>=2^3;
- p>=5 gives log_2(p)>=9/4, since 5^4>=2^9;
- p>=7 gives log_2(p)>=5/2, since 7^2>=2^5.

There is at most one p_i=2 and at most one p_i=3. Relative to the common
baseline 9/4, their possible deficits are at most 5/4 and 3/4 respectively.
Therefore

S >= 9ell/4 - 2.                                      (1)

If at least one p_i is outside {2,3,5}, primality gives p_i>=7, improving
that term's baseline by at least 1/4. In that case

S >= 9ell/4 - 2 + 1/4.                                (2)

## Four or more distinct primes

If ell>=4, (1) gives S>=2ell-1, hence also S>=ell. Every term in the
displayed expression for D is nonnegative. Thus D>=0 for all exponents
in this case.

## Three distinct primes

Here (1) gives S>=19/4.

If some exponent is at least 3, then some x_i>=1 and X>=s_i>=1. Since
S-3>0, the global lower bound gives

D >= 4S(S-5)+4(S-3)
  = 4((S-2)^2-7)
  >= 4((11/4)^2-7) = 9/4 > 0.

If all exponents equal 2, then X=H=0 and D=4S(S-5). If the prime set is
not {2,3,5}, it contains a prime at least 7, and (2) yields S>=5. Thus
D>=0. The sole remaining configuration is prime set {2,3,5} with all
exponents 2, giving r=900, one of the stated exceptions.

## Two distinct primes

If the prime set is not {2,3}, then S>=3: in the absence of 2, each
prime is at least 3 and contributes at least 3/2; if 2 occurs, the other
prime is at least 5 and S>=1+9/4>3. Hence S>=2ell-1 and the global
nonnegative-term bound again gives D>=0.

For prime set {2,3}, write a=a_2>=2, b=a_3>=2, and t=log_2(3). Then

D(a,b)=b^2*t(t-1)+2ab*t-4a-4b*t.

We have t>=11/7, since 3^7=2187>=2048=2^11. Thus t>=1 and
2bt-4>=0. The following exact factorizations prove the needed monotonic
comparisons without a derivative argument:

D(a,b)-D(2,3)
 = (a-2)(2bt-4)+(b^2-9)t(t-1) >=0,      if b>=3;

D(a,b)-D(4,2)
 = (a-4)(2bt-4)+(b^2-4)t(t-1)+4(b-2)t >=0, if a>=4.

The two bases are positive:

D(4,2)=4(t^2+t-4)>=8/49>0,
D(2,3)=9t(t-1)-8>=4/49>0.

The bounds use monotonicity of these quadratics for t>=11/7 and their
values at 11/7. Since a,b are integers at least 2, all configurations
are covered except (a,b)=(2,2) and (3,2), giving r=36 and r=72.

## One or zero distinct primes

For ell=1, D=a_1^2*s_1*(s_1-1)>=0. For ell=0, the original products
both equal 1. This covers all source ranges without a boundary exception.

## The three exceptions really fail

For r=36, S=1+t and D=4(1+t)(t-2)<0, since 1<3<4 gives 0<t<2.

For r=72, D=4(t^2-3)<0. Indeed 3^3=27<32=2^5 gives t<5/3, and
(5/3)^2<3.

For r=900, all exponents are 2 and S=log_2(30) lies strictly between
0 and 5 because 1<30<32. Thus D=4S(S-5)<0.

Since all quantities being logged are positive, strict negativity of D
is strict failure of the original inequality. The exact exception set
is therefore {36,72,900}.
