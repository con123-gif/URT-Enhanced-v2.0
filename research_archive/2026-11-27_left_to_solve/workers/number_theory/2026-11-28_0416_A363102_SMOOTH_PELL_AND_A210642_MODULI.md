## A363102: explicit counterexample and corrected infinite statement
Source https://oeis.org/A363102/internal revision 46 (17 Aug 2026), cached exact entry: McEachen's 5 Aug 2024 comment conjectures positions of denominator 1 match A060515(m) for m>2. https://oeis.org/A060515 defines its terms n>1 by all prime factors of n²−2 being ≤n, offset 1. Its initial list 2,10,28,39,45,54,58 shows A060515(7)=58. But 58²−2=3362=2*41². The published exact denominator formula for the literal reciprocal right-fold continued fraction with negative tail −2 is
A(n)=(n²−2)/gcd(n²−2,n!/2), n>=3.
Since 41 is prime and 41<=58<82, its valuation in 58!/2 is exactly one; the factor 2 cancels. Thus A(58)=41, not 1. Exact rational evaluation independently confirms this. This refutes the supplementary assertion even after the first two smooth indices are excluded. It does NOT refute the two prime-denominator/multiplicity conjectures.

Corrected all-index characterization: for n>=3, A(n)=1 iff every prime factor of n²−2 is ≤n and there is no prime p satisfying n²−2=2p².
Proof using established prior results: A(n) divides n²−2, and any prime factor >n survives. Conversely, when all prime factors are ≤n, the known prime-or-one theorem implies that any A(n)>1 is a prime p≤n. The published U_prime_constraints theorem (TrureTuring dossier scope item 3) then gives n²=2p²+2. Conversely, for a prime solution of that equation with n>=3, necessarily p odd, n>=4 and p<n<2p (p=2 gives 10 nonsquare). Hence n!/2 has exactly one factor p and contains 2. The denominator formula gives A(n)=p. QED.
Prior overlap is explicit: https://www.trureturing.org/research/bouras/ quotes Gracia Llorente Theorem 5.12, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23506866, and explicitly lists (41,58) as an already computed extra occurrence. ## A210642: exact endpoint completion of existing reduction, not a full conjecture resolution
Source https://oeis.org/A210642/internal revision 22, 30 May 2026. Let G(n,m) mean m>1 and n! is incongruent modulo m to every k! for 1<=k<n. The source claims least such m is prime <=2n apart from n=4,6. For n>=7 and composite m<=2n, the exact classification is
G(n,m) iff n is prime and m=2n.

Proof: If m<2n has distinct factors 2<=u<v, then v<=m/2<n, hence m divides (n−1)!. The sole remaining form is m=p² for prime p. For p=2 use distinct factorial factors 2,4; p=3 use 3,6. For p>=5, p²<2n implies n−1>=(p²−1)/2>=2p, so p and 2p are distinct factorial factors and p² divides (n−1)!. In each case (n−1)! and n! are both zero modulo m, excluding G.
For m=2n and composite n having an odd prime divisor p, the distinct factors p and 2n/p are both <=n−1: p<=n/2 and 2n/p<=2n/3, and the latter is even. If n is a power of 2, n=8 is handled by 2*4*6=48 divisible by 16, and n>=16 by distinct factors 4,n/2. Thus m=2n fails for composite n. For prime n, n divides n! but no earlier positive factorial; hence G(n,2n) holds and G(n,n) also holds. QED.
For n>=2, a modulus m<n cannot qualify because k=m gives collision of zero residues. Hence for n>=7, the prime condition on the minimum follows automatically from the upper bound a(n)<=2n. At prime n the minimum is n. The only remaining original mathematical issue is existence, at every composite n>=7, of a prime in (n,2n) avoiding all the earlier factorial residues. Bertrand's theorem alone does not imply that avoidance.
