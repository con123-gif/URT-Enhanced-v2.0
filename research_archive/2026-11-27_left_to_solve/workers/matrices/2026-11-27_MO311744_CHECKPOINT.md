## Exact new mathematical result: fixed-basis reading of MO311744

Source asks about a real diagonalizable A with strictly positive eigenvalues,
given an orthogonal T such that the symmetric part of T^T A T has a positive
diagonal r-by-r leading block and a trailing block with zero diagonal. It asks
for Delta=diag(delta I_r,Delta_2), delta>0, Delta_2 nonnegative diagonal,
with (T^T A T) Delta + Delta (T^T A T)^T positive semidefinite.

The words “given the above orthogonal T” support the fixed-basis reading.
An alternative existential-choice-of-T interpretation is not refuted below;
the related author question https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/2940954
explicitly asks for existence of a special T. Do not submit a faithful-statement
claim until the quantifiers are confirmed against the official brief.

General exact criterion: write B=T^T A T and d the diagonal of Delta. For a
real symmetric PSD matrix, a zero diagonal forces that whole row and column
to be zero: restrict the quadratic form to two coordinates and vary the
coordinate having zero square coefficient. Consequently the entire scaling
question in the GIVEN basis is equivalent to the linear equations
B_ij d_j + B_ji d_i = 0 whenever either i or j lies in the trailing block,
with d_i=delta>0 on the leading block and d_j>=0 elsewhere. Necessity follows
from PSD zero diagonal. Sufficiency follows because the resulting matrix has
a positive diagonal leading block and an identically zero trailing block and
cross-block. By homogeneity delta can be normalized to 1.

Counterexample, n=3, r=2, T=I:
A = [[20,0,8],[0,28,8],[-3,-15,0]].
Its characteristic polynomial is (x-8)(x-16)(x-24); the three positive
eigenvalues are distinct. Explicit diagonalization:
V=[[-10,-6,2],[-6,-2,-2],[15,3,1]],
W=[[1/32,3/32,1/8],[-3/16,-5/16,-1/4],[3/32,-15/32,-1/8]].
V W = W V = I, det V=128, A V = V diag(8,16,24).
The leading symmetric block is diag(20,28), the trailing diagonal is 0,
and the positive diagonal sum is exactly trace A = 48.

For d=delta>0, t>=0 and Delta=diag(d,d,t), direct multiplication gives
Q=[[40*d,0,8*t-3*d],[0,56*d,8*t-15*d],[8*t-3*d,8*t-15*d,0]].
Let c0=8*t-3*d, c1=8*t-15*d. Test the quadratic forms on
v0=(c0,0,-20*d-1), v1=(0,c1,-28*d-1). Their values are exactly
-2*c0^2 and -2*c1^2. Positive semidefiniteness forces both c0=c1=0;
subtracting gives 12*d=0, contradiction. This holds even if t is allowed
arbitrary real. No numerical eigenvalue estimate is involved.

Exact executed independent arithmetic, Python/SymPy:
```python
import sympy as s
A=s.Matrix([[20,0,8],[0,28,8],[-3,-15,0]])
V=s.Matrix([[-10,-6,2],[-6,-2,-2],[15,3,1]])
W=V.inv()
print(A.charpoly().as_expr().factor(),V.det(),W)
assert A*V==V*s.diag(8,16,24)
assert V*W==W*V==s.eye(3)
d,t=s.symbols('d t')
D=s.diag(d,d,t); Q=A*D+D*A.T
print('Q=',Q)
for i in (0,1):
    c=Q[i,2]; a=Q[i,i]; v=s.zeros(3,1)
    v[i]=c; v[2]=-(a/2+1)
    assert s.expand((v.T*Q*v)[0]+2*c*c)==0
    print('cross',i,c,'test',list(v))
print('all exact witness identities verified')
```
