## Full rank-one classification (human proof)
Source https://mathoverflow.net/questions/298064/trace-of-symmetric-matrices-in-fixed-rank, April17 2018, asks whether for all finite fields the cardinality of rank-t symmetric matrices of trace zero is at least that of any specified nonzero trace. Source comments by Richard Stanley and Singh point to prior general-matrix counting. The actual canonical statement blob9eafe0a76d8482d458cdeb3b9aa400b1418111ac and proof blob48cdff6cc7e4a92333ffbc417dabacd768eb632c checked against the source wording: no size or characteristic restriction. The rank-one size-one F2 disproof is valid; the proof uses empty/nonempty families and retains all quantifiers. Prior inline check is archived, not rerun here.

A stronger result classifies the difference D=|S_a(1,m)|-|S_0(1,m)| for m>=1, a!=0 in F_q:
* q even or m odd: D=1.
* q odd and m even: D=1-chi(-1)^(m/2) q^(m/2), where chi is the quadratic character.
This is an independent human derivation, NOT an extra accepted problem or a new Lean certificate.

Proof: each rank-one symmetric matrix has unique image line L and is c vv^T for a normalized representative v of L and unique c!=0. Indeed rank one gives v w^T and symmetry implies w proportional to v. Put Q(v)=sum v_i^2 and N_m=number of vectors with Q(v)=0 including zero. There are (N_m-1)/(q-1) isotropic lines. Each gives q-1 matrices of trace zero, and each anisotropic line gives exactly one matrix of trace a, since c Q(v)=a. Thus |S_0|=N_m-1, |S_a|=(q^m-N_m)/(q-1), and D=1+(q^m-q N_m)/(q-1). For even q, Q(v)=(sum v_i)^2 and N_m=q^(m-1). For odd q write e=chi(-1). The number of pairs x,y with x^2+y^2=b is q-e for b!=0 and q+(q-1)e for b=0. To see this for b!=0, count y^2=x^2-b using (x-y)(x+y)=b; there are q-1 pairs since 2 invertible, hence sum_x chi(x^2-b)=-1. Multiplication by e gives sum_x chi(b-x^2)=-e. For b=0 count the origin and the nonzero x each with 1+e choices of y. Consequently N_(m+2)=(q-e)q^m+qe N_m, or N_(m+2)-q^(m+1)=qe(N_m-q^(m-1)). Bases N_1=1 and N_2=q+(q-1)e prove the claimed formula. In particular arbitrary odd dimensions refute the conjecture over every finite field; restricting to even dimensions or odd characteristic alone does not repair it (e.g. q=3,m=2). The inequality does hold for rank one when q odd, m even and e^(m/2)=1, so do not overstate the disproof.

## Prior literature warning
The 2024 Beelen–Johnsen–Singh paper https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05936v1, introduction, explicitly covers fixed-rank fixed partial trace counts in odd characteristic (take k=m, delta=1). It is a more general prior result, and the rank-one counting method is elementary. The 1980 Seroussi–Lempel factorization paper https://doi.org/10.1137/0209059 is related background.
