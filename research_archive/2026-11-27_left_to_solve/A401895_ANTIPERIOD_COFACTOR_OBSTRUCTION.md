## Fixed-width notation and theorem

Let R_h(x) reverse an h-digit decimal word, retaining leading zero positions before reversal; 0 <= x < 10^h. Let h >= 1, B = 10^h, t >= 3 odd, T = B^t, and let K satisfy

    1 <= K < B,    K mod10 in {7,8}.

Set

    G = (T+1)/(B+1),    n = K G.

The quotient is integral because t is odd. Then the fixed-width ht reversal r of n cannot satisfy

    3 n r = 2(T^2-1).

For an actual ht-digit candidate, the fixed-width reversal is its ordinary decimal reversal.

## Exact reversal calculation

The base-B blocks of n, from left to right and with their full width retained, are

    K-1, B-K, K-1, B-K, ..., B-K, K.

Define q = R_h(K-1). Since the last digit of K is7 or8, incrementing K-1 to K changes only its last decimal digit. Therefore

    R_h(K) = q + B/10.

Digit complementation also gives

    R_h(B-K) = B-1-q.

Reverse the block order and reverse each individual block. Comparison with the analogous expansion of (q+1)G gives the exact identity

    r = (q+1)G + T/10 - 1.

This is a finite-width identity for every h and every odd t, not an eventual-period approximation. In particular,

    10(B+1)r = (10q+B+11)T + 10(q-B).

## Integer coefficient obstruction

Assume 3nr = 2(T^2-1). Substitute n = K(T+1)/(B+1) and the exact expression for r, and cancel T+1 > 0. Define the integers

    E = 20(B+1)^2 - 3K(10q+B+11),
    F = 20(B+1)^2 - 30K(B-q).

The assumed product is exactly equivalent to

    E T = F.

Because 0 <= q < B and 1 <= K < B,

    -10B^2 < F < 20(B+1)^2,

hence |F| < 20(B+1)^2. If h >= 2, then B >= 100 and

    20(B+1)^2 < B^3 <= T.

Thus |F| < T. The integer equation E T = F forces E = F = 0. Their two zero equations imply

    3K(10q+B+11) = 30K(B-q).

Cancel 3K > 0:

    10q+B+11 = 10(B-q),

or 20q = 9B-11. The left side is even and the right side is odd, a contradiction.

If h=1, B=10, the same bound works for t>=5 since 20*11^2 < 10^5. The only remaining t is3 and K is7 or8. Here q=K-1 and the two (E,F) pairs are

    K7: (719,1580),    K8: (236,1700).

Neither satisfies 1000E=F. This completes all parameter cases.

## Cofactor consequence at every candidate length

Write a hypothetical remaining counterexample with length m and T=10^m as the standard cofactor decomposition

    3ab+1=T,    cd=T+1,    n=ac,    r=2bd,

where a,b,c,d are positive integers. Both n and r have length m, and their endpoint digits are7 and8. This decomposition follows from coprime allocation of the two odd factors (T-1)/3 and T+1; it is existing prior work in the live notes.

Suppose the multiplicative order of10 modulo d is smaller than2m. Since d | T+1, the residue of10^m is-1 modulo d. Also d>1: n/(T+1)=a/d is strictly between0 and1. In the cyclic subgroup generated by10, its order is2h, 10^h=-1, and m/h is a positive odd integer. No cyclicity of the entire unit group modulo a composite modulus is assumed. Smaller order means h<m and therefore t=m/h>=3.

Because d | (10^h+1),

    K = a(10^h+1)/d

is integral, and n=K(B^t+1)/(B+1). The inequality n<T yields K<=B; the units condition K congruent n modulo10 excludes K=B. Hence 1<=K<B and K ends in7 or8. The theorem just proved gives a contradiction. Applying the same argument to r=2b(T+1)/c proves

    ord_d(10) = ord_c(10) = 2m.

The related minus-denominator statements follow from the ALREADY KNOWN no-repeated-block theorem. The four fractions are reduced because the two odd factors T-1 and T+1 are coprime and T+1 is coprime to3:

    n/(T-1)=c/(3b),    r/(T-1)=2d/(3a),
    n/(T+1)=a/d,       r/(T+1)=2b/c.

A proper period of either minus-denominator would make n or r a nontrivial repeated decimal block. Consequently every hypothetical counterexample must satisfy all four exact order conditions

    ord_(3a)(10) = ord_(3b)(10) = m,
    ord_c(10) = ord_d(10) = 2m.

Only the plus-denominator anti-period argument is the additional candidate contribution in this note; the repeated-word theorem and its minus-period consequence are explicitly credited to prior work.

## What remains open

Maximal multiplicative orders do not by themselves force a single prime factor to have full order: an lcm of smaller prime-power orders can give the same modulus order. Thus there is no justified contradiction for all prime allocations. No proof that the surviving decimal words have a shorter anti-period has been given. The unrestricted equation n*reverse(n)=(2/3)(10^(2m)-1) remains open here.

The new condition is an all-length cyclotomic/cofactor sieve, not a reformulation counted as a solution. Formalization and any independent novelty audit are still separate work. No verifier branch was changed.
