## A. Integer right inverse (complete answer, standard Smith normal form)
Source: https://www.openproblemgarden.org/op/inverse_of_an_integer_matrix
For full row rank m x n integer M, an integral right inverse exists iff gcd of all m x m minors is 1. Smith normal form U M V = [diag(s_1,...,s_m) 0], with U,V unimodular, shows surjectivity iff every s_i=1; the gcd equals their product. The suggested rowwise gcd condition is necessary but insufficient: M=[[2,0,1,1],[0,2,1,1]], with diagonal block 2I and row gcds 1, has equal rows mod 2 and cannot have a right inverse mod 2. This answers the question, not a claim of novelty.

## B. Retkes square-summable series (complete proof and sharp constant; previously solved)
Source: https://www.openproblemgarden.org/op/inequality_for_square_summable_complex_series (2013 solution by anonymous).
Every n>=1 uniquely equals 2^k(2l+1), k,l>=0. By Cauchy-Schwarz on each disjoint sector, |sum_l a_(2^k(2l+1))/(l+1)|^2 <= (pi^2/6) sum_l |a_(2^k(2l+1))|^2. Summation proves the target, including absolute convergence of inner sums. Taking a_(2l+1)=1/(l+1), even terms zero, gives equality. The optimal constant is 6/pi^2. Standard harmonic block decomposition, not a new solution to an open conjecture.

## C. Bounded real power series (complete infinite coefficient criterion, not a finite decision algorithm)
Source: https://www.openproblemgarden.org/op/criterion_for_boundedness_of_power_series
Let f(x)=sum_{n>=0} a_n x^n be a real-coefficient power series converging everywhere on R. For R a positive integer and j>=0 define b_j(R)=2/(j+1) if j even, zero otherwise, and c_j(R)=sum_{n>=0} a_n R^n b_(n+j)(R) (the b notation is independent of R). The series converges absolutely. Then f is bounded on R iff there is a finite M>=0 such that for every positive integer R and every nonnegative integer d, both real symmetric matrices
   [ M b_(i+j) + c_(i+j)(R) ]_{0<=i,j<=d}
   [ M b_(i+j) - c_(i+j)(R) ]_{0<=i,j<=d}
are positive semidefinite.
Proof: b_j is integral of t^j on [-1,1], c_j is integral of t^j f(Rt) there, by uniform convergence. The quadratic forms are integrals of (M +/- f(Rt)) p(t)^2. Necessity immediate. If M +/- f(Rt) is negative anywhere, continuity gives an interval on which it is strictly negative, and a nonzero continuous bump supported there has strictly negative weighted-square integral. By Weierstrass approximation, a real polynomial sufficiently uniformly close to the bump also has negative weighted-square integral, contradiction. Thus |f(Rt)|<=M for all t in [-1,1] and all R. All real x are covered. This is an explicit coefficient-only infinite semidefinite criterion, not necessarily the effective finite test the questioner may have hoped for. No priority claim.
The page also asks whether every bounded entire real-axis function is a*cos(g(x)) with g entire and real on R. Counterexample exp(-x^2): if a is nonzero and equality holds for real x then by the identity theorem exp(-z^2)=a*cos(g(z)) everywhere. The exponential has no zeros, so g omits every pi/2+k*pi (k integer). Little Picard implies g constant, contradiction. If a=0 equality is impossible. This refutes the literal single-cosine representation, not every algebra generated by cosines.

## D. Bilu–Linial two-sided signing bound (complete Platonic family; partial general conjecture)
Source: https://www.openproblemgarden.org/op/signing_a_graph_to_have_small_magnitude_eigenvalues
For each graph of a Platonic solid, exhibit real symmetric signed adjacency A with spectral radius < 2 sqrt(d-1). For the cube, use the recursion below. For the other graphs, the following exact signs are in the order specified. The sign on a listed edge is applied in both directions. All other entries zero.

Tetrahedron: vertices 0..3, edges (01,02,03,12,13,23), signs (-,-,-,-,-,+).
Octahedron: vertices 0..5, edges (01,02,03,04,12,13,15,24,25,34,35,45), signs (-,-,-,-,-,+,-,+,+,-,+,-). Missing pairs are 05,14,23, so this is K_(2,2,2).
Dodecahedron: generalized Petersen G(10,2), vertices i and 10+i, i modulo 10. Outer cycle (i,i+1) signs (-,+) for even/odd i; inner edges (10+i,10+(i+2 mod 10)) signs (-,+) for even/odd i; spokes (i,10+i) all negative.
Icosahedron: poles 0,11, upper u_i=1+i, lower v_i=6+i, i modulo 5. Six edge orbits (0,u_i),(11,v_i),(u_i,u_(i+1)),(v_i,v_(i+1)),(u_i,v_i),(u_i,v_(i-1)); signs (-,-,-,+,-,+).
Exact sandbox verification: constructed these integer matrices and used SymPy LDL decomposition of B=4(d-1)I-A^2. All diagonal pivots strictly positive; min pivot / determinant respectively: tetrahedron 21/5,441; cube 5,390625; octahedron 8,262144; dodecahedron 323/98,2468267229184; icosahedron 57/7,1212855082209. Positivity implies the stated strict spectral bounds. Approximate radii respectively sqrt(5),sqrt(3),2,2.5816293276680757,1+sqrt(6).
Icosahedron exact characteristic polynomial: (x^2-2x-5)(x^2+2x-5)(x^4-8x^2+11)^2. Dodecahedron: (x^2-2x-1)(x^2+2x-1)(x^8-12x^6+43x^4-52x^2+16)^2.
Reproducible certificate procedure: construct matrices from edge lists above; use sympy.Matrix, set A[i,j]=A[j,i]=sign, B=4*(d-1)*eye(A.rows)-A*A; L,D=B.LDLdecomposition(hermitian=False); assert L*D*L.T==B and all(x>0 for x in D.diagonal()). This procedure is the exact algebra used in the sandbox, not a Lean kernel check.

## E. Recursive extension (infinitely many graphs)
For the k-cube let C_0=[0], J_0=[1] and recursively C_(k+1)=I_2 tensor C_k + X tensor J_k, J_(k+1)=Z tensor J_k, where X=[[0,1],[1,0]], Z=diag(1,-1). By induction C_k is a signed adjacency of Q_k, J_k^2=I, J_k C_k=-C_k J_k, C_k^2=kI. The cross terms cancel at each step. Hence rho(C_k)=sqrt(k), which meets Bilu–Linial for k>=2 (the conjecture's usual meaningful degree range).
More generally, if G is d-regular with signed adjacency A satisfying rho(A)<=2sqrt(d-1), d>=2, the signed adjacency of Q_k square G defined as T=C_k tensor I + J_k tensor A satisfies T^2=kI + I tensor A^2. Thus rho(T)^2=k+rho(A)^2<= k+4(d-1)<=4(d+k-1). This proves the two-sided signing bound for each Platonic graph times any hypercube, and any other base graph with a valid signing. This uses standard anticommuting-matrix algebra; originality not established. Not a solution of the unrestricted graph-signing conjecture.
